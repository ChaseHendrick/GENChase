### Verdict

The note needs a major revision before it is circulated.
- **What holds up:** The mathematics is correct. Proposition 1 and the torus corollary check line by line. Every computation I reran reproduced bit for bit, and my own code reproduces the headline Matern window exponents to about 1e-13.
- **What is not new:** The central point, that a single exponent fitted over a finite rank window is not a test of an asymptotic bound, is elementary. It is largely anticipated by several sources:
  - Stringer's own SI Example 3: with equal tuning radius the spectrum is flat until the radius is resolved.
  - Pospisil and Pillow, who say the tail's form "remains unclear".
  - A 2022 paper showing that Stringer's exponents depend on the chosen range.
  - The kernel-matrix eigenvalue literature.
  The note cites none of these.
- **What is overstated:** Several worded claims go beyond what the outputs show, or are contradicted by the note's own outputs:
  - A crossing claim fails with 8,704 neurons.
  - The claim that "nu = 0.75 exceeds the bound" rests on three choices that the note does not report: a single-slope fit over ranks 11-500, unwhitened coordinates, and P = 2,800.
  - The Matern MEME paragraph fits the wrong target spectrum.
  - The intro says Section 2 proves that "no function" of the spectrum identifies the decay. It proves something weaker.
- **Fairness:** The note is fair to Stringer in quoting "Consistent with the hypothesis". It omits their stronger sentences, which are the real target. It understates Pospisil and Pillow's claim about MEME.

### Must-fix

1. **results.tex:38 and :44, and make_numbers.py:84-87.** The text says the border code lies "above it in every set for ell >= 1 at d = 8" and that "Finite populations do not change this."
   - The crossing is computed only for infinitely many neurons.
   - `out/matern_finiteN.json` with 8,704 neurons contradicts it. 8D MP034 0915, nu = 1, ell = 1 gives a mean of 1.2424 (2.5-97.5%: 1.2400-1.2446), entirely below 1.25. 8D MP030 0607 gives 1.2502 (1.2479-1.2524), sitting on the bound.
   - The claim that border codes reach 1.49 "in four of the six 8D sets" (results.tex:38, discussion.tex:1) is also fragile. MP032 0915 reaches it only at ell = 8, with 1.492. Border shifts at ell = 4 are about -0.011 to -0.017, and ell = 8 was not run at finite N. So likely only three sets reach it with 8,704 neurons.
   - Fix: restate the crossing and the "reach" counts at finite N, or restrict them explicitly to N = infinity.

2. **abstract note.tex:40, results.tex:40, discussion.tex:1.** "Non-differentiable codes with nu = 0.75 exceed the bound" is true only for one fit, one metric and one P. None of the following is reported.
   - **(a) Sub-window.** All 29 nu = 0.75 cells above the bound over ranks 11-500 are below it over ranks 101-500 (the note's own `w101_500` field). At ell = 4, d = 8 they span 1.125-1.241 against 1.25; at d = 4, 1.433-1.443 against 1.50. The spectra are strongly curved: the ranks 11-100 minus ranks 101-500 exponent has a median of 0.21 for ell >= 1 at d = 8, and ranges from -0.76 to 0.83 over all cells. `make_numbers.py:101-102` computes `SubAOneOneHundred` and `SubAHundredFiveHundred`, but the text never uses them. A reader could reject these codes as stand-ins for the near-straight reported spectra.
   - **(b) Metric.** In whitened coordinates of the same 8 parameters, nu = 0.75 at ell = 4 falls below 1.25 in all four 8D sets I tested: MP034 0915 1.2017, MP033 0822 1.1675, MP030 0607 1.2070, MP032 0810 1.1784. Border codes at ell = 8 drop from 1.628 to 1.332 (MP033 0822) and from 1.464 to 1.377 (MP030 0607). The 4D sets are nearly unaffected: MP034 0920 goes from 1.5165 to 1.5265.
   - **(c) Stimulus count.** For 8D MP034 0915, nu = 0.75, ell = 4, the exponent is 1.2127 at P = 1400, 1.2483 at P = 2000 and 1.2823 at P = 2800 (one random subset each). It crosses the bound between 2,000 and 2,800 stimuli. For 4D MP034 0920 it is 1.4828 at P = 1400 and 1.5165 at P = 2800.
   - The result stands only as an existence statement in the chosen metric. The note should say so, report the sub-windows and the dependence on P, and separate finite-P effects from pre-asymptotic effects at d = 4 and 8, as it already does for d = 1.
   - The long-ell exponents behave like 1 + 2 nu / d_eff with d_eff about 2.4-3.5 in the 8D sets. The nominal d = 8 overstates the dimension resolved at 2,800 stimuli, and this is part of what drives the effect.

3. **results.tex:83 and meme_analyze.py:125-126.** The Matern MEME fits use the exact moments of the 2,800-stimulus sample spectrum, padded with zeros to 8,704. MEME's eigenmoment estimates are unbiased for the population signal covariance over the stimulus distribution. For these codes that spectrum is nonzero beyond rank 2,800 and differs from m_n at high ranks. The note's own Table 3 shows the padding is not harmless: "zero beyond rank 2,800" alone shifts alpha2 by +0.054. So the counts "13 differentiable below" and "6 nu = 0.75 above" do not describe what MEME would return from these codes. Either recompute with population spectra or relabel and demote the paragraph.

4. **note.tex:49.** "Section 2 shows that no function of the noise-free spectrum of finitely many stimuli identifies the asymptotic decay" is not what is proved.
   - The corollary gives delta-close spectra, not equal ones, so a discontinuous function is not excluded.
   - It is proved only on a torus with uniform measure and Fourier eigenfunctions (C = sqrt 2).
   - Say "no function continuous in the spectrum", or "no estimator robust to arbitrarily small perturbations", and state the scope.
   - Exact equality is in fact trivial: change the code away from the P stimuli. That argument is not the one given.
   - The abstract (note.tex:40) should also say "bounded eigenfunctions (for example the torus)".

5. **Prior work and novelty (bibliography, note.tex:119-132).** The central point is largely anticipated, and the note must position itself against:
   - **Stringer SI Example 3 (stringer_SI.txt:418-425).** Equal tuning radius gives a spectrum that is "approximately flat until the eigenfunctions' spatial scale matches this radius". This is the length-scale effect that Section 3 presents.
   - **Pospisil and Pillow.** Main text (PMC12625980.txt:55): "it remains unclear whether the tail of the eigenspectrum decays as a power law". PMC12625980.txt:38 claims that MEME "can infer eigenvalues beyond the rank of the data even if there is no relationship with early eigenvalues", and :115 says the parametric form can be checked by the eigenmoment misfit. The note's MEME section bears directly on both claims and should quote them, not only the Fig. S1 caption.
   - **The kernel eigenvalue literature.** Prop 1 is Weyl's inequality plus a tail bound on a positive semidefinite matrix. It is an elementary special case of the finite-sample kernel eigenvalue bounds with bounded eigenfunctions and a tail term, for example Braun, JMLR 7 (2006); Koltchinskii and Gine (2000); Shawe-Taylor et al. (2005).
   - **"Bayesian interpolation for power laws in neural data analysis" (arXiv 2204.08525).** It reports that Stringer's exponent estimates depend heavily on "the range of data chosen", and that individual natural-image recordings fall on both sides of the critical value.
   - After these, what the note adds is modest: the Matern computations on the real coordinates, the d = 1 aliasing split, and the MEME far-tail sensitivity.

### Mathematics (item 1 of your list)

- **Proposition 1** is correct.
  - PSD tails give ||B - B'|| <= max(||B||, ||B'||).
  - ||B|| <= tr B <= P C^2 tau_M.
  - H is a projection, and Weyl's inequality closes the argument.
  - C is defined as a uniform bound, and it is finite (sqrt 2) wherever the proposition is invoked: the torus and the circle.
  - It is never applied to the Matern codes on R^d, where the eigenfunctions need not be bounded. The discussion scopes it correctly ("bounded eigenfunctions such as Fourier modes"); the intro (note.tex:49) does not.
- **The corollary** is correct.
  - The lattice count gives eta_j ~ 4 pi^2 (j/omega_d)^(2/d).
  - The tail integral is L^(1-b)/(a-1).
  - The separation is sum lambda_j eta_j infinite for a < 1 + 2/d and finite for a' > 1 + 2/d.
- **Matern nu = 1** is at the border in every d. The integral of |w|^2 against the spectral density scales as the integral of r^(1-2nu), which diverges logarithmically at nu = 1. The window exponent 1 + 2nu/d equals 1 + 2/d there. The only gap is the torus-versus-R^d justification noted above.
