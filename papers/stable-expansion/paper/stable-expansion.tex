\begin{abstract}
Point vortices can move self-similarly, keeping their shape while the configuration grows like $\sqrt{t}$ and turns. For three vortices such expanding configurations are stable, and Zbarsky used this to show that vortex patches placed at the three vortices stay confined for all time; he wrote that the result would most likely carry over to stably growing systems of four or more vortices, given sufficiently good stability. We give such configurations of four and five vortices, with the circulations $(-1, -5/2, -1/9, 4/5)$ and $(-1, 3/7, 7/8, -9/7, -47/35)$. In similarity variables their linearization has, besides the double eigenvalue $0$ of the rotation and of the one-parameter family of such configurations, only eigenvalues with real part $-1$ or $-2$. The proof is computer-assisted: the configurations are enclosed by the Krawczyk method in ball arithmetic, and the spectrum is controlled through an exact lemma, which shows that six eigenvalues are forced by the rotation, the scaling, the translations and the family and that the others come in pairs $k$, $2 - k$, together with enclosures of traces of powers of the Jacobian. The stability is nonlinear: every motion with the same circulations that starts near one of the two configurations stays within a bounded distance of an exactly self-similar expansion of a nearby member of the family, the member with the same energy, and an a priori estimate of the same kind holds for approximate solutions whose error is small and decays faster than the velocities. With this estimate in place of the one step of Zbarsky's proof that needs three vortices, his confinement theorem holds for the two configurations: vortex patches placed at the vortices stay within distance $\varepsilon t^{1/4 + \varepsilon}$ of their centres of vorticity for all time, and the centres stay within a bounded distance of an exactly self-similar expansion of a single member of the family. The proof carries his argument over to any number of patches, with every estimate written out. Linear stability is an open condition; in a random sample about one four-vortex collapse in seven and one five-vortex collapse in seventeen reverses into a linearly stable expansion.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} point vortices; self-similar motion; nonlinear stability; vortex patches; computer-assisted proof; interval arithmetic.\\
\textbf{MSC 2020:} 76B47, 35Q31, 37N10, 65G20.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

$N$ point vortices with circulations $\Gamma_j$ at positions $z_j \in \C$ move by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bs}
  \overline{\dot z_j} = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{k \ne j}\frac{\Gamma_k}{z_j - z_k}.
\end{equation}
A motion is self-similar when the configuration keeps its shape while it grows or shrinks and turns; this happens when $\dot z_j = \kappa(z_j - z_c)$ for all $j$ at $t = 0$, with one $\kappa \in \C$, where $z_c = \sum_j\Gamma_jz_j/\sum_j\Gamma_j$ \cite[Sect.~2]{mw2026}. Then the size of the configuration grows or shrinks like $|1 + 2t\re\kappa|^{1/2}$, the configuration expands when $\re\kappa > 0$ and collapses in finite time when $\re\kappa < 0$ \cite{kimura1987, ns1979, aref2010, gotoda2021, ks2022}. Reversing every circulation reverses time, so each collapse becomes an expansion of the same shape, and conversely.

Zbarsky \cite{zbarsky2021} proved that for a generic self-similarly expanding configuration of three vortices one may replace each vortex by a small patch of vorticity and the patches stay confined for all time, their radius growing more slowly than their distance. The restriction to three vortices comes from the stability analysis. He writes that ``if one found stably growing systems of 4 or more vortices, the confinement result would most likely carry over with little modification. However, one needs sufficiently good stability results; orbital linear stability, which may not be hard to obtain for some systems, is not enough'' \cite[pp.~4--5 of the arXiv version]{zbarsky2021}; the same point recurs in \cite[after Cor.~16]{zbarsky2024}. For three vortices, stability of the expanding family goes back to Tavantzis and Ting \cite{tt1988}; Iwayama and Yajima studied the linear stability of the self-similar motions of three vortices in the $\alpha$-models \cite{iy2023}. Leoncini, El Kettani and Ugalde \cite{leu2026} split a vortex into three or four vortices that expand from a point by reversing a collapse, inside a larger system of vortices, and observe numerically, with a first-order argument for times of order the inverse of the relative size of the new vortices, that the other vortices do not feel the split; they do not study the stability of the expanding shape.

We show that stable expansions of four and five vortices exist, and that Zbarsky's confinement theorem holds for them. Section~\ref{sec:setting} sets up similarity variables, in which a self-similar motion is a fixed point, and proves a lemma on the structure of the spectrum of the linearization (Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum}): six eigenvalues are forced by the rotation, the scaling, the translations and the one-parameter family of self-similar configurations with the given circulations (the second eigenvalue $2$ through a pairing), and the others come in pairs $k$, $2 - k$. For three vortices nothing else is left. For four vortices one pair is left, and a single number decides its stability; for five, two pairs. Section~\ref{sec:results} states and proves, with a computer-assisted proof, that there are configurations of four and of five vortices whose reversal expands with every other exponent of real part $1$ (Theorems~\ref{thm:four} and~\ref{thm:five}, Corollary~\ref{cor:expansion}). Section~\ref{sec:nonlinear} proves that the stability is nonlinear, with the sharp rate and a limit fixed by the energy (Theorem~\ref{thm:nonlinear}, Corollary~\ref{cor:physical}), together with an a priori estimate for approximate solutions (Proposition~\ref{prop:forced}). Section~\ref{sec:patches} uses that estimate in place of the one step of Zbarsky's proof that needs three vortices and obtains confinement of vortex patches for the two configurations (Theorem~\ref{thm:patches}). Section~\ref{sec:numerics} gives numerical illustrations, controls and a sample of how common such configurations are, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses what is and is not shown.

\section{Similarity variables and the structure of the spectrum}\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Similarity variables}

Let $V(\zeta) = (V_1, \ldots, V_N)$ be the velocity given by \eqref{eq:bs}, $V_j = (i/2\pi)\sum_{k \ne j}\Gamma_k/\overline{(\zeta_j - \zeta_k)}$. It is homogeneous of degree $-1$, $V(\lambda\zeta) = \lambda^{-1}V(\zeta)$ for real $\lambda > 0$, and equivariant under rotations, $V(e^{i\phi}\zeta) = e^{i\phi}V(\zeta)$. For $\kappa = a + i\Omega$ put $z_j(t) = \rho(t)e^{i\Omega\tau}\zeta_j(\tau)$ with $\rho^2 = 1 + 2at$ and $d\tau/dt = \rho^{-2}$; then \eqref{eq:bs} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sim}
  \frac{d\zeta}{d\tau} = V(\zeta) - \kappa\zeta ,
\end{equation}
and a self-similar motion with this $\kappa$ and $z_c = 0$ is a fixed point of \eqref{eq:sim}. We fix the scale of time by $2\pi a = -1$ for a collapse, write $2\pi\kappa = -1 + ib$, and work with $E(\zeta) = 2\pi(V(\zeta) - \kappa\zeta) = 2\pi V(\zeta) + (1 - ib)\zeta$ and the rescaled time $s = \tau/2\pi$; for a collapse, $s = -\ln\rho$. The winding number of the collapse is $P = |b|/2$ \cite{mw2026}. Let $DE$ be the real $2N \times 2N$ Jacobian of $E$ at a zero $\zeta^*$. A perturbation along an eigenvector with eigenvalue $k$ behaves like $e^{ks} = \rho^{-k}$ relative to the size $\rho$ of the configuration. Writing $\delta\zeta = u + iv$, one finds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DE}
  DE = \begin{pmatrix} A_r + I & A_i + bI\\ A_i - bI & -A_r + I\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_{jk} = \frac{i\Gamma_k}{\overline{(\zeta_j - \zeta_k)}^{\,2}}\ (k \ne j),\quad A_{jj} = -\sum_{k \ne j}A_{jk},
\end{equation}
with $A = A_r + iA_i$ evaluated at $\zeta^*$.

\emph{Time reversal.} If $\zeta^*$ is a collapse for the circulations $\Gamma$, it is a fixed point of the similarity dynamics of the circulations $-\Gamma$ with $\kappa$ replaced by $-\kappa$, an expansion with the same winding, and the Jacobian of its similarity dynamics is $-DE$; the similarity time is then $s = \ln\rho$. So a mode with eigenvalue $k$ of $DE$ behaves in the expansion like $\rho^{-k}$ relative to its size, and it decays as the configuration grows exactly when $\re k > 0$.

\subsection{The structure of the spectrum}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:spectrum}
Let $\zeta^*$ be a zero of $E$, with all $\Gamma_j \ne 0$ and the $\zeta_j^*$ distinct, and suppose that near $(\zeta^*, b)$ the zeros $(\zeta, b)$ of $E$, with the circulations fixed, form up to rotation a smooth curve through $(\zeta^*, b)$ whose $\zeta$-component is transverse to the rotations. Then the characteristic polynomial $p$ of $DE$ satisfies $p(\lambda) = p(2 - \lambda)$ and is divisible by
\[
  \lambda^2(\lambda - 2)^2\bigl((\lambda - 1)^2 + b^2\bigr),
\]
and the quotient $q$, of degree $2N - 6$, also satisfies $q(\lambda) = q(2 - \lambda)$. In particular the $2N - 6$ remaining eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, come in pairs $k$, $2 - k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Rotation.} $E(e^{i\phi}\zeta) = e^{i\phi}E(\zeta)$, so differentiating at $\phi = 0$ gives $DE(i\zeta^*) = iE(\zeta^*) = 0$.

\emph{Scaling.} $E(\lambda\zeta) = 2\pi V(\zeta)/\lambda + (1 - ib)\lambda\zeta$; at $\lambda = 1$ the derivative is $-2\pi V(\zeta^*) + (1 - ib)\zeta^* = 2(1 - ib)\zeta^*$, since $2\pi V(\zeta^*) = -(1 - ib)\zeta^*$. So $DE\,\zeta^* = 2\zeta^* - 2b\,i\zeta^*$, and on the span of $\zeta^*$ and $i\zeta^*$ the map $DE$ is triangular with eigenvalues $2$ and $0$.

\emph{Translations.} $V$ is invariant under $\zeta \mapsto \zeta + c(1, \ldots, 1)$, so $DE$ acts on $\mathbf 1 = (1, \ldots, 1)$ and $i\mathbf 1$ as multiplication by $1 - ib$: $DE\,\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1 - b\,i\mathbf 1$, $DE(i\mathbf 1) = i\mathbf 1 + b\mathbf 1$, with eigenvalues $1 \pm ib$.

\emph{The family.} Along the curve of zeros, $E(\xi(\sigma); b(\sigma)) = 0$, and $\partial_bE = -i\zeta$. Differentiating, $DE\,\xi'(0) = b'(0)\,i\zeta^*$, so $DE^2\xi'(0) = 0$. As $\xi'(0)$ is not a real multiple of $i\zeta^*$, the generalized kernel of $DE$ has dimension at least $2$: the algebraic multiplicity of $0$ is at least $2$.

\emph{Pairing.} With $H = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j \ne k}\Gamma_j\Gamma_k\ln|\zeta_j - \zeta_k|$ and $I = \sum_j\Gamma_j|\zeta_j|^2$, the field $\zeta \mapsto 2\pi V(\zeta) - ib\zeta$ is the Hamiltonian vector field of $H + (b/2)I$ for the symplectic form $\sum_j\Gamma_j\,dx_j \wedge dy_j$, which is nondegenerate because every $\Gamma_j \ne 0$ (Kirchhoff's form of \eqref{eq:bs} and the rotations generated by $I$, up to the sign convention of the form). The linearization of a Hamiltonian vector field at any point is an infinitesimally symplectic matrix, whose characteristic polynomial is even. Since $DE$ is this linearization plus the identity, $p(\lambda) = r(\lambda - 1)$ with $r$ even, that is $p(\lambda) = p(2 - \lambda)$, with multiplicities.

\emph{Conclusion.} By pairing, the multiplicity of $2$ equals that of $0$, at least $2$, so $\lambda^2(\lambda - 2)^2$ divides $p$. The span of $\mathbf 1$ and $i\mathbf 1$ is invariant, and the characteristic polynomial of $DE$ on it, $(\lambda - 1)^2 + b^2$, divides $p$ too; its roots $1 \pm ib$ are never $0$ or $2$, so the two factors are coprime and $p$ is divisible by the displayed product. That product is invariant under $\lambda \mapsto 2 - \lambda$, hence so is the quotient.
\end{proof}

For three vortices $2N - 6 = 0$: in similarity variables every exponent of a three-vortex expansion is forced by the symmetries and the family, which is the linear shadow of the classical stability of the expanding three-vortex family \cite{tt1988}. For four vortices one pair $k$, $2 - k$ remains, and with $c = k(2 - k)$ the sum of squares of all eigenvalues gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c}
  \tr(DE^2) = \bigl(0 + 0 + 4 + 4 + 2(1 - b^2)\bigr) + (4 - 2c),\qquad c = 3 + 3b^2 - \tr(A\bar A),
\end{equation}
using $\tr(DE^2) = 2\tr(A\bar A) + 2N(1 - b^2)$, which follows from \eqref{eq:DE}. Both remaining exponents have positive real part exactly when $c > 0$; when $c > 1$ they are $1 \pm i\sqrt{c - 1}$. For five vortices two pairs remain; with $u = (k - 1)^2$ they are the roots $u_1$, $u_2$ of $u^2 - m_1u + m_2$, where $m_1 = u_1 + u_2$ and $u_1^2 + u_2^2$ follow from $\tr((DE - I)^2)$ and $\tr((DE - I)^4)$ after the contributions $4 - 2b^2$ and $4 + 2b^4$ of the six forced eigenvalues are removed. Every remaining exponent has positive real part exactly when every root $u$ satisfies $|\re\sqrt{u}| < 1$; in particular it suffices that both roots are real and less than $1$.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\begin{theorem}[Four vortices]\label{thm:four}
For the circulations $\Gamma = (1, 5/2, 1/9, -4/5)$ there is a self-similar collapse of four Euler point vortices with $2\pi\kappa = -1 + ib$, winding
\[
  P = b/2 = 2.5258464741344909107270\ldots,
\]
and $z_4$ with real part $8/25$ in the gauge $z_1 \in \R$, $z_c = 0$, whose Jacobian $DE$ has the six eigenvalues $0$, $0$, $2$, $2$, $1 \pm ib$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum} and the pair $1 \pm i\vartheta$ with $\vartheta = 4.4588620962481\ldots$; here $c = 20.881451193358\ldots$ in \eqref{eq:c}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Five vortices]\label{thm:five}
For the circulations $\Gamma = (1, -3/7, -7/8, 9/7, 47/35)$ there is a self-similar collapse of five Euler point vortices with winding $P = 2.40234703541713841\ldots$ and $z_2$ with real part $3/5$ in the same gauge, whose Jacobian $DE$ has the six eigenvalues of Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum} and the four eigenvalues $1 \pm 3.4538555\ldots i$ and $1 \pm 3.1885675\ldots i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:expansion}
For the circulations $(-1, -5/2, -1/9, 4/5)$ and $(-1, 3/7, 7/8, -9/7, -47/35)$ the configurations of Theorems~\ref{thm:four} and~\ref{thm:five} expand self-similarly, growing like $\sqrt{t}$. In similarity variables the linearization at them has the double eigenvalue $0$, whose generalized eigenspace consists of the rotation and the tangent of the one-parameter family of expanding configurations with the same circulations, and every other eigenvalue has real part $-1$ or $-2$. In the invariant complement of the rotation and the family, every solution of the linearized equation therefore decays, relative to the size $\rho$ of the configuration, at least like $\rho^{-1}$, that is like $t^{-1/2}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the theorems]
The program \texttt{verify\_\allowbreak stable\_\allowbreak expansion.py} (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}) works in the gauge of \cite[Sect.~8]{mw2026}: $\Gamma_1 = 1$, $z_1$ real, $\sum_k\Gamma_k/(z_j - z_k) = \lambda\overline{z_j}$ with $\lambda = 2P - i$, which is $E = 0$ with $b = 2P$ and $z_c = 0$. For four vortices it fixes $x_4 = 8/25$, $\Gamma_2 = 5/2$ and $\Gamma_3 = 1/9$ and proves by the Krawczyk test, in the ball arithmetic of FLINT/Arb, that the $8$ equations have exactly one zero in a box of radius $10^{-4}$ around the numerical solution; on the box the circulations are nonzero, the total circulation is nonzero, the vortices are distinct and away from the origin, and the necessary conditions $\sum_j\Gamma_jz_j = 0$, $\sum_{j<k}\Gamma_j\Gamma_k = 0$ and $\sum_j\Gamma_j|z_j|^2 = 0$ are enclosed with $0$. The second of these forces $\Gamma_4 = -4/5$. The Jacobian of the square system is invertible on the box, so by the implicit function theorem the zeros with $\Gamma_2$, $\Gamma_3$ fixed form a smooth curve parametrized by $x_4$; along it $\Gamma_4$ stays $-4/5$, and its tangent $v$ has $\im v_1 = 0$ and $v_4$ with real part $1$, while $i\zeta^*$ has $\im(i\zeta_1^*) = x_1 \ne 0$, so $v$ is not a real multiple of $i\zeta^*$. The hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum} hold, and the program also checks on the box that the vectors of its proof satisfy their identities. It then encloses $c = 3 + 3b^2 - \tr(A\bar A)$ by $20.8814511933587 \pm 1.4\cdot10^{-14}$, so $c > 1$ and the remaining pair is $1 \pm i\sqrt{c - 1}$; a second enclosure from $\tr((DE - I)^2)$ agrees. For five vortices it fixes $x_2 = 3/5$ and $\Gamma_2$, $\Gamma_3$, $\Gamma_4$, proves existence the same way, finds $\Gamma_5 = 47/35$, checks the hypotheses of the lemma, and encloses $m_1$ and $m_2$; the discriminant $m_1^2 - 4m_2$ is positive and both roots, $u_1 = -11.929118\ldots$ and $u_2 = -10.166963\ldots$, are negative, so the four remaining eigenvalues are $1 \pm i\sqrt{-u_1}$ and $1 \pm i\sqrt{-u_2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the corollary]
By the time reversal of Section~\ref{sec:setting} the configurations expand for the negated circulations, with Jacobian $-DE$ in similarity variables and similarity time $s = \ln\rho$. The eigenvalues of $-DE$ are $0$, $0$, $-2$, $-2$, $-1 \mp ib$ and, by the theorems, $-1 \mp i\vartheta$ or $-1 \mp i\sqrt{-u_{1,2}}$. By the theorems no remaining eigenvalue is $0$, so the generalized eigenspace of $0$ is exactly two-dimensional and, by the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum}, spanned by $i\zeta^*$ and the tangent of the family. The eigenvalues with real part $-1$ are simple: $\vartheta \ne b$ for four vortices and $\sqrt{-u_1}$, $\sqrt{-u_2}$ and $b$ are distinct for five, which the program checks. The eigenvalue $-2$ is double, the partner of the one at $0$, and even as a Jordan block it contributes only $s\,e^{-2s}$. On the complementary invariant subspace every solution of the linearized equation therefore decays at least like $e^{-s} = \rho^{-1}$, and $\rho^2$ grows linearly in $t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Open sets]\label{rem:open}
The conclusions are open conditions: $c$, and the roots $u$, depend continuously on the configuration and the circulations along the collapse set, which near these points is a smooth manifold of dimension $N - 1$ (modulo translations, rotations and the scalings of lengths and circulations) \cite[Sect.~7]{mw2026}. So every collapse near those of the theorems, with nearby circulations, reverses into a linearly stable expansion as well; the stable expansions of four and five vortices form open subsets of the $(N - 1)$-dimensional collapse set, not isolated examples.
\end{remark}

\section{Nonlinear stability}\label{sec:nonlinear}

Throughout this section $\zeta^*$ is the configuration of Theorem~\ref{thm:four} or of Theorem~\ref{thm:five}, $b^*$ is its $b$, and the circulations are negated, so that $\zeta^*$ expands. By the time reversal of Section~\ref{sec:setting}, a motion $z(t)$ of the vortices with circulations $-\Gamma$ is $z(t) = \rho(t)e^{-ib^*s}\zeta(s)$ with $\rho^2 = 1 + t/\pi$, $s = \ln\rho$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp}
  \frac{d\zeta}{ds} = -E(\zeta),\qquad E(\zeta) = 2\pi V(\zeta) + (1 - ib^*)\zeta,
\end{equation}
where $V$ and $E$ are formed with the circulations $\Gamma$ of the theorems; this holds for every motion, not only for self-similar ones. We use the Euclidean norm $|\cdot|$ on $\C^N = \R^{2N}$, write $\mathcal O = \{e^{i\phi}\zeta^*: \phi \in \R\}$ for the orbit of $\zeta^*$ under rotations, and $H = -\sum_{j<k}\Gamma_j\Gamma_k\ln|\zeta_j - \zeta_k|$ for the energy, which is the same for $\Gamma$ and $-\Gamma$. By the proof of the theorems, the zeros of $E(\zeta; b) = 2\pi V(\zeta) + (1 - ib)\zeta$ near $(\zeta^*, b^*)$ with $z_1$ real form a real-analytic curve $\sigma \mapsto (\xi(\sigma), b(\sigma))$, $|\sigma| < \sigma_1$, with $\xi(0) = \zeta^*$ and $b(0) = b^*$, parametrized by $\sigma = x_4 - 8/25$ or $\sigma = x_2 - 3/5$. Since $E(\zeta; b^*) = E(\zeta; b) + i(b - b^*)\zeta$, the solution of \eqref{eq:exp} starting at $\xi(\sigma)$ is $e^{-i(b(\sigma) - b^*)s}\xi(\sigma)$: another self-similar expansion, with a different shape and winding. We write $\xi'$ for the derivative in $\sigma$ and $\zeta'$ for the derivative of a solution in $s$. We fix $\sigma_0 \in (0, \sigma_1)$ once, small enough for Lemma~\ref{lem:coords} below and such that $H(\xi(\sigma))$ is strictly monotone for $|\sigma| \le \sigma_0$; the latter is possible because the program encloses $dH(\xi(\sigma))/d\sigma$ at $\sigma = 0$ away from $0$ ($-0.5133915789\ldots$ for four vortices and $0.2322290983\ldots$ for five).

\begin{theorem}[Nonlinear stability]\label{thm:nonlinear}
There are $\delta_0 > 0$ and $C$ such that every solution of \eqref{eq:exp} with $\delta := \operatorname{dist}(\zeta(0), \mathcal O) \le \delta_0$ exists for all $s \ge 0$, and there are $\sigma_\infty$ with $|\sigma_\infty| \le C\delta$ and $\phi_\infty \in \R$ with
\[
  \bigl|\zeta(s) - e^{i(\phi_\infty - (b(\sigma_\infty) - b^*)s)}\xi(\sigma_\infty)\bigr| \le C\delta e^{-s}\qquad (s \ge 0).
\]
Moreover $\sigma_\infty$ is the unique $\sigma$ with $|\sigma| \le \sigma_0$ and $H(\xi(\sigma)) = H(\zeta(0))$.
\end{theorem}

The constants $\delta_0$ and $C$ are not explicit.

\begin{corollary}[In the original variables]\label{cor:physical}
Let $z(t)$ be a motion of $N$ point vortices with the circulations $-\Gamma$ and $|z(0) - e^{i\phi}\zeta^*| \le \delta \le \delta_0$ for some $\phi$. Then there are a member $\xi(\sigma_\infty)$ of the family, with the energy $H(z(0))$, and $\phi_\infty \in \R$ such that the exactly self-similar expansion $Z(t) = \rho(t)e^{i(\phi_\infty - b(\sigma_\infty)\ln\rho(t))}\xi(\sigma_\infty)$ satisfies $|z(t) - Z(t)| \le C\delta$ for all $t \ge 0$. The vortices stay within a bounded distance of an exactly self-similar expansion whose size grows like $\sqrt t$, and the relative deviation is $O(t^{-1/2})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $\zeta(0) = z(0)$. Multiplying the estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:nonlinear} by $\rho e^{-ib^*s} = e^{s}e^{-ib^*s}$ gives $|z(t) - Z(t)|\le C\delta$. $Z$ is a motion because $\xi(\sigma_\infty)$ is a zero of $E(\cdot\,; b(\sigma_\infty))$, so a fixed point of the similarity dynamics with $b(\sigma_\infty)$ in place of $b^*$, and $H(\xi(\sigma_\infty)) = H(\zeta(0))$ by the theorem.
\end{proof}

The theorem follows from an a priori estimate for approximate solutions, whose constants do not depend on how long the approximate solution is followed; that is the form a continuation argument needs (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\begin{proposition}[Forced version]\label{prop:forced}
For $\mu > 0$ and $0 < \nu < \min(\mu, 1)$ there are $\delta_1 > 0$ and $C_1$, depending only on $\zeta^*$, $\mu$ and $\nu$, with the following property. Let $0 < S \le \infty$ and let $\zeta: [0, S) \to \C^N$ be continuously differentiable with
\[
  \bigl|\zeta'(s) + E(\zeta(s))\bigr| \le \alpha e^{-\mu s}\quad(0 \le s < S),\qquad \delta := \operatorname{dist}(\zeta(0), \mathcal O),\qquad \delta + \alpha \le \delta_1.
\]
Then for $0 \le s < S$ we have $\operatorname{dist}(\zeta(s), \mathcal O) \le C_1(\delta + \alpha)$, and the distance from $\zeta(s)$ to $\{e^{i\phi}\xi(\sigma): \phi \in \R,\ |\sigma| \le \sigma_0\}$ is at most $C_1(\delta + \alpha)e^{-\nu s}$. If $S = \infty$, there are $\sigma_\infty$ with $|\sigma_\infty| \le C_1(\delta + \alpha)$ and a continuous function $\phi$ with $|\zeta(s) - e^{i\phi(s)}\xi(\sigma_\infty)| \le C_1(\delta + \alpha)e^{-\nu s}$. If $S = \infty$ and $\mu > 1$, there is moreover $\phi_\infty \in \R$ with
\[
  \bigl|\zeta(s) - e^{i(\phi_\infty - (b(\sigma_\infty) - b^*)s)}\xi(\sigma_\infty)\bigr| \le C_1(\delta + \alpha)e^{-s}\qquad(s \ge 0).
\]
\end{proposition}

Suppose that $z$ satisfies $\dot z = V_{-\Gamma}(z) + e(t)$ for $t \ge 0$, where $V_{-\Gamma}$ is the velocity \eqref{eq:bs} for the circulations $-\Gamma$ and $e$ is a continuous forcing with $|e(t)| \le K(1 + t)^{-(1+\mu)/2}$. Then $\zeta(s) = \rho^{-1}e^{ib^*s}z(t)$ satisfies $\zeta' = -E(\zeta) + 2\pi\rho e^{ib^*s}e(t)$, and since $1 + t \ge \rho^2$ the forcing is at most $2\pi Ke^{-\mu s}$: the proposition applies with $\alpha = 2\pi K$ when $\delta + 2\pi K \le \delta_1$. So the error must be small and decay faster than the velocities, which are of order $t^{-1/2}$.

That a manifold of equilibria whose transverse spectrum lies in the open left half-plane attracts every nearby solution exponentially, each to one of its equilibria, is classical \cite{aulbach1984}. We give the short proof because the family is a curve of equilibria only modulo the rotation, and because we need the forced version and the sharp rate.

\begin{lemma}[Coordinates]\label{lem:coords}
Let $W_0 = \operatorname{span}_\R\{i\zeta^*, \xi'(0)\}$ and let $W_1$ be the sum of the generalized eigenspaces of $DE$ at $\zeta^*$ for its nonzero eigenvalues, and fix a linear isomorphism $Q: \R^{2N-2} \to W_1$. For $\sigma_0$, $r_0 > 0$ small enough, $\Phi(\phi, \sigma, y) = e^{i\phi}(\xi(\sigma) + Qy)$ is a real-analytic diffeomorphism from $(\R/2\pi\mathbb Z) \times (-\sigma_0, \sigma_0) \times \{|y| < r_0\}$ onto an open neighborhood $U$ of $\mathcal O$. In these coordinates a continuously differentiable $\zeta(s) = \Phi(\phi, \sigma, y) \in U$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coords}
  (\phi', \sigma', y') = \bigl(h(\sigma, y), f(\sigma, y), g(\sigma, y)\bigr) + L_{\sigma, y}^{-1}\bigl(e^{-i\phi}(\zeta' + E(\zeta))\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $L_{\sigma,y}(p_1, p_2, w) = p_1\,i(\xi(\sigma) + Qy) + p_2\,\xi'(\sigma) + Qw$ and $(h, f, g)(\sigma, y) = -L_{\sigma,y}^{-1}E(\xi(\sigma) + Qy)$ are real-analytic, with $h(\sigma, 0) = -(b(\sigma) - b^*)$, $f(\sigma, 0) = 0$ and $g(\sigma, 0) = 0$. The spectrum of $B(\sigma) = \partial_yg(\sigma, 0)$ consists of the eigenvalue $-2$, of algebraic multiplicity $2$, and of $2N - 4$ simple eigenvalues on the line $\re\lambda = -1$, and there is $K_B$ with $\|e^{B(\sigma)s}\| \le K_Be^{-s}$ for $s \ge 0$ and $|\sigma| \le \sigma_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:expansion} the generalized kernel of $DE$ is $W_0$, so $\C^N = W_0 \oplus W_1$ with both summands invariant under $DE$. The differential of $\Phi$ at $(0, 0, 0)$ is $L_{0,0}$, which is invertible; by rotation equivariance the differential of $\Phi$ is invertible at every $(\phi, \sigma, y)$ with $\sigma$, $y$ small. If $\Phi(\phi, \sigma, y) = \Phi(\tilde\phi, \tilde\sigma, \tilde y)$ with small $\sigma$, $\tilde\sigma$, $y$, $\tilde y$, then $e^{i(\tilde\phi - \phi)}\zeta^*$ is close to $\zeta^*$, so $\tilde\phi - \phi$ is close to $2\pi\mathbb Z$ because $\zeta^* \ne 0$, and the inverse function theorem at $(0, 0, 0)$ gives equality. This proves the first claim. Differentiating $\zeta = \Phi(\phi, \sigma, y)$ gives $\zeta' = e^{i\phi}L_{\sigma,y}(\phi', \sigma', y')$, and $E(\zeta) = e^{i\phi}E(\xi(\sigma) + Qy)$ by equivariance, which gives \eqref{eq:coords}. At $y = 0$, $E(\xi(\sigma)) = i(b(\sigma) - b^*)\xi(\sigma) = L_{\sigma, 0}(b(\sigma) - b^*, 0, 0)$, which gives the values of $h$, $f$ and $g$ there.

For the spectrum, consider \eqref{eq:coords} without forcing, with $b^*$ replaced by $b(\sigma)$ for a fixed $\sigma$. Rotation equivariance shows that this changes only the equation for $\phi$, by an additive constant, and $\xi(\sigma)$ becomes a fixed point. Its linearization in the coordinates is block upper triangular with diagonal blocks $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & *\\ 0 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and $B(\sigma)$, because $f(\cdot, 0) = g(\cdot, 0) = 0$, and it is similar to $-DE(\xi(\sigma); b(\sigma))$. Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum} applies at every point of the curve, so the spectrum of $B(\sigma)$ consists of $-2$, $-2$, $-1 \pm ib(\sigma)$ and the negatives of the remaining eigenvalues. At $\sigma = 0$ these lie on $\re\lambda = -1$ and are simple, by the theorems. Along the curve they stay there for small $|\sigma|$: for four vortices $c > 1$ is preserved, and for five a positive discriminant and two negative roots $u$ are preserved, while being distinct is an open condition. The eigenvalue $-2$ keeps algebraic multiplicity $2$ because the others stay away from it. So
\[
  e^{B(\sigma)s} = \sum_{j=1}^{2N-4}e^{\lambda_j(\sigma)s}\Pi_j(\sigma) + e^{-2s}\bigl(\mathrm{Id} + sM(\sigma)\bigr)\Pi_0(\sigma),
\]
where the $\Pi_j$ are the spectral projections of the simple eigenvalues $\lambda_j$, $\re\lambda_j = -1$, $\Pi_0$ is that of $-2$ and $M = (B + 2)\Pi_0$ satisfies $M^2 = 0$. The projections are continuous in $\sigma$, so $K_B$ exists after shrinking $\sigma_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:forced}]
Choose $\gamma \in (\nu, 1)$ with $\gamma \ne \mu$. The spectrum of $B(0)$ lies in $\re\lambda \le -1$, so there is an inner product on $\R^{2N-2}$, with norm $|\cdot|_*$, in which $\langle B(0)y, y\rangle_* \le -\frac{1 + \gamma}{2}|y|_*^2$. Since $B(\sigma) \to B(0)$ and $g(\sigma, y) = B(\sigma)y + O(|y|^2)$, there are $\sigma_2 \in (0, \sigma_0]$ and $r_2 > 0$, depending on $\gamma$, such that $\langle g(\sigma, y), y\rangle_* \le -\gamma|y|_*^2$, $|f(\sigma, y)| \le C_f|y|_*$ and $\|L_{\sigma,y}^{-1}\| \le C_L$ for $|\sigma| \le \sigma_2$ and $|y|_* \le r_2$, a part of the domain of Lemma~\ref{lem:coords}. By the inverse function theorem and equivariance, $\operatorname{dist}(\zeta(0), \mathcal O) = \delta$ gives $\zeta(0) = \Phi(\phi_0, \sigma(0), y(0))$ with $|\sigma(0)| + |y(0)|_* \le C_0\delta$ when $\delta$ is small.

Let $S_1 \le S$ be the supremum of the $s_1$ such that $\zeta(s) \in \Phi(\R \times [-\sigma_2/2, \sigma_2/2] \times \{|y|_* \le r_2/2\})$ for $0 \le s \le s_1$. On $[0, S_1)$ the forcing term in \eqref{eq:coords} has size at most $C_L\alpha e^{-\mu s}$ (in the norm $|\cdot|_*$, after enlarging $C_L$), so the upper right Dini derivative of $|y|_*$ satisfies $D^+|y|_* \le -\gamma|y|_* + C_L\alpha e^{-\mu s}$; where $y = 0$ this holds because $y'$ is then the forcing term alone. The comparison lemma gives
\[
  |y(s)|_* \le e^{-\gamma s}|y(0)|_* + C_L\alpha\int_0^se^{-\gamma(s - \tau)}e^{-\mu\tau}\,d\tau \le C_0\delta e^{-\gamma s} + \frac{C_L\alpha}{|\gamma - \mu|}e^{-\min(\gamma, \mu)s} \le C_2(\delta + \alpha)e^{-\nu s}.
\]
Then $|\sigma'| \le C_f|y|_* + C_L\alpha e^{-\mu s} \le C_3(\delta + \alpha)e^{-\nu s}$, so $|\sigma(s) - \sigma(0)| \le C_3(\delta + \alpha)/\nu$. If $\delta + \alpha$ is so small that $C_0\delta + C_3(\delta + \alpha)/\nu < \sigma_2/4$ and $C_0\delta + C_2(\delta + \alpha) < r_2/4$, then $\zeta(s)$ stays in the image of $\R \times [-\sigma_2/4, \sigma_2/4] \times \{|y|_* \le r_2/4\}$ on $[0, S_1)$, a compact set inside the image of $\R \times (-\sigma_2/2, \sigma_2/2) \times \{|y|_* < r_2/2\}$, and by continuity $S_1 = S$. The two distance bounds follow, since $|\Phi(\phi, \sigma, y) - e^{i\phi}\zeta^*| \le |\xi(\sigma) - \zeta^*| + |Qy|$ and $|\Phi(\phi, \sigma, y) - e^{i\phi}\xi(\sigma)| = |Qy|$. If $S = \infty$, $\sigma(s)$ converges to some $\sigma_\infty$ with $|\sigma(s) - \sigma_\infty| \le C_3(\delta + \alpha)e^{-\nu s}/\nu$, and $|\zeta(s) - e^{i\phi(s)}\xi(\sigma_\infty)| \le |\xi(\sigma(s)) - \xi(\sigma_\infty)| + |Qy(s)|$.

For the sharp rate, let $\mu > 1$ and $S = \infty$; by what was shown with $\nu = 1/2$ (after decreasing $\delta_1$ if necessary), $|\sigma(s) - \sigma_\infty| + |y(s)| \le C(\delta + \alpha)e^{-s/2}$. Write $y' = B(\sigma_\infty)y + r(s) + F(s)$, where $r = (B(\sigma(s)) - B(\sigma_\infty))y + (g(\sigma, y) - B(\sigma)y)$ satisfies $|r(s)| \le C(\delta + \alpha)e^{-s/2}|y(s)|$ and $F$, the forcing term of \eqref{eq:coords}, satisfies $|F(s)| \le C_L\alpha e^{-\mu s}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:coords} and the variation of constants formula,
\[
  e^{s}|y(s)| \le K_B|y(0)| + \frac{K_BC_L\alpha}{\mu - 1} + K_BC(\delta + \alpha)\int_0^se^{-\tau/2}e^{\tau}|y(\tau)|\,d\tau,
\]
and Gronwall's inequality gives $|y(s)| \le C(\delta + \alpha)e^{-s}$. Then $|\sigma(s) - \sigma_\infty| \le \int_s^\infty(C_f|y| + C_L\alpha e^{-\mu\tau})\,d\tau \le C(\delta + \alpha)e^{-s}$. With $\beta_\infty = b(\sigma_\infty) - b^*$, $\phi' = h(\sigma, y) + O(\alpha e^{-\mu s}) = -\beta_\infty + O((\delta + \alpha)e^{-s})$, so $\phi(s) + \beta_\infty s$ converges to some $\phi_\infty$ at the rate $(\delta + \alpha)e^{-s}$, and the estimate follows from $\zeta(s) = e^{i\phi(s)}(\xi(\sigma(s)) + Qy(s))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:nonlinear}]
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:forced} with $\alpha = 0$, $\mu = 2$ and $\nu = 1/2$ to the solution on its maximal interval of existence. It stays in a compact set without collisions, so it exists for all $s \ge 0$, and the last part of the proposition gives the estimate, with $|\sigma_\infty| \le C\delta$.

For the energy: the sum $\sum_{j<k}\Gamma_j\Gamma_k$ vanishes exactly for the circulations of the theorems, so $H(\lambda\zeta) = H(\zeta)$ for $\lambda > 0$; with the invariance of $H$ under rotations and its conservation by \eqref{eq:bs}, $H(\zeta(s))$ is constant along \eqref{eq:exp}. As $e^{-i\phi(s)}\zeta(s) \to \xi(\sigma_\infty)$, $H(\xi(\sigma_\infty)) = H(\zeta(0))$. For $\delta_0$ small, $|\sigma_\infty| \le C\delta_0 \le \sigma_0$, and since $\sigma_0$ was chosen so that $H(\xi(\sigma))$ is strictly monotone on $|\sigma| \le \sigma_0$, $\sigma_\infty$ is determined by $H(\zeta(0))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the energy varies along the family]\label{rem:energy}
Differentiating the identity $\nabla H(\zeta)\cdot E(\zeta) = 0$, which expresses the conservation of $H$ and its invariance under rotations and scalings, at a zero of $E$ shows that $\nabla H(\zeta^*)$ is a left null vector of $DE$; it is not zero, since $2\pi V$ is, up to sign, the Hamiltonian vector field of $H$ for the nondegenerate form $\sum_j\Gamma_j\,dx_j \wedge dy_j$ (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum}) and $2\pi V(\zeta^*) = -(1 - ib^*)\zeta^* \ne 0$. When $b'(0) \ne 0$, the kernel of $DE$ is spanned by $i\zeta^*$ alone, and the range of $DE$ is $W_1 \oplus \R\,i\zeta^*$, a hyperplane that does not contain $\xi'(0)$. As $\nabla H(\zeta^*)$ vanishes on that range, $dH/d\sigma = \nabla H(\zeta^*)\cdot\xi'(0) \ne 0$. The program encloses $b'(0)$ away from $0$ as well ($0.94234865\ldots$ and $-9.6795840\ldots$) and checks that $\nabla H(\zeta^*)^{\mathsf T}DE$ contains $0$. The limit of a perturbed expansion is therefore fixed by a conserved quantity, which Section~\ref{sec:numerics} illustrates.
\end{remark}

\section{Vortex patches}\label{sec:patches}

We now replace the point vortices by patches of vorticity. The Euler equations $\partial_t\omega + u\cdot\nabla\omega = 0$, $u = K*\omega$, $K(x) = x^\perp/(2\pi|x|^2)$, move point vortices by \eqref{eq:bs}, and have a unique global weak solution for bounded, compactly supported initial vorticity \cite{yudovich1963}; a solution $\omega(t) = \sum_j\omega_j(t)$ is the sum of the parts transported from the initial pieces $\omega_j$. Let $\zeta^*$, $b^*$ and $\Gamma$ be as in Section~\ref{sec:nonlinear}, and put $Y = \zeta^*/\sqrt\pi$. With the origin of time moved to the point of emergence, $\rho^2 = t/\pi$, the expansion of Corollary~\ref{cor:physical} is $x^*(t) = \sqrt t\,e^{-i(b^*/2)\ln(t/\pi)}Y$, a motion of point vortices with circulations $-\Gamma$. Let $\mathcal V = \{z \in \C^N: \sum_j\Gamma_jz_j = 0\}$, which contains $Y$ and $iY$, and let $S_Y = \{z \in \mathcal V: \langle Y, z\rangle = |Y|^2\}$ with $\langle z, w\rangle = \sum_j\overline{z_j}w_j$, a real affine slice of dimension $2N - 4$ through $Y$, transverse to rotations and dilations. Every $x \in \mathcal V$ with $\langle Y, x\rangle \ne 0$ is $x = e^{i\beta}\gamma z$ with unique $\beta \in \R/2\pi\mathbb Z$, $\gamma > 0$ and $z \in S_Y$, namely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice}
  e^{i\beta}\gamma = \langle Y, x\rangle/|Y|^2,\qquad z = |Y|^2x/\langle Y, x\rangle,
\end{equation}
and writing $x = \lambda e^{i\phi}(Y + w)$ with $\lambda > 0$ and $|w| \le |Y|/2$ one finds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slicebound}
  |z - Y| \le 4|w|,\qquad |\gamma/\lambda - 1| \le |w|/|Y|.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Confinement of vortex patches]\label{thm:patches}
Let $\zeta^*$ be the configuration of Theorem~\ref{thm:four} or of Theorem~\ref{thm:five}, with $Y$ and $S_Y$ as above. Let $\varepsilon > 0$ be small enough, $M > 0$ and $r > 0$. There is $T$ such that for every $t_0 > T$ the following holds. Let $\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_N$ be bounded functions of definite sign with $\operatorname{supp}\omega_j \subseteq B(\sqrt{t_0}\,Y_j, r)$, $\|\omega_j\|_{L^{2+\varepsilon}} \le M$, $\int\omega_j = -\Gamma_j$ and $\sum_j\int x\,\omega_j(x)\,dx = 0$. Let $\omega(t) = \sum_j\omega_j(t)$, $t \ge t_0$, be the solution of the Euler equations with $\omega(t_0) = \sum_j\omega_j$, and let $\bar x_j(t) = -\Gamma_j^{-1}\int x\,\omega_j(t, x)\,dx$ be the centres of vorticity. Then for all $t \ge t_0$ and all $j$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\omega_j(t)$ has the sign of $-\Gamma_j$ and $\operatorname{supp}\omega_j(t) \subseteq B(\bar x_j(t), \varepsilon t^{1/4 + \varepsilon})$;
\item[(ii)] $\bar x(t) = e^{i\beta(t)}\gamma(t)z(t)$ with $\beta$, $\gamma$ and $z(t) \in S_Y$ given by \eqref{eq:slice}, and $|z(t) - Y| < \varepsilon$, $|\gamma(t)/\sqrt t - 1| < \varepsilon$;
\item[(iii)] there are $\sigma_\infty$ with $|\sigma_\infty| < \varepsilon$, $\phi_\infty \in \R$ and a constant $C$ such that the exactly self-similar expansion $Z(t) = \sqrt{t/\pi}\,e^{i(\phi_\infty - (b(\sigma_\infty)/2)\ln(t/\pi))}\xi(\sigma_\infty)$ of point vortices with circulations $-\Gamma$ satisfies $|\bar x(t) - Z(t)| \le C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Parts (i) and (ii) are the conclusion of Zbarsky's theorem for three vortices \cite[Theorem~2]{zbarsky2021}, with his generic expanding triangle replaced by the configurations of Theorems~\ref{thm:four} and~\ref{thm:five}. Part (iii) adds that the centres of vorticity stay within a bounded distance of an exactly self-similar expansion of a single member of the family. Dávila, del Pino, Musso and Parmeshwar \cite{dpmp2024} construct solutions whose continuous vorticity stays for all time in balls of fixed radius $3\varepsilon$ around the points of an expanding three-vortex spiral, a stronger confinement than $t^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ and one that needs no stability; they remark that their construction applies to the four- and five-vortex spirals of Novikov and Sedov \cite{ns1979} under analogous conditions on the circulations. That is a construction of particular solutions; Theorem~\ref{thm:patches} is about every initial datum of the stated kind.

\begin{proof}
The proof is Zbarsky's bootstrap argument \cite[Sects.~3--5]{zbarsky2021} for $N$ patches, and Appendix~\ref{app:patches} proves the estimates it needs in full. Fix $\varepsilon \le 1/18$ small enough, then an even integer $n$ large enough depending on $\varepsilon$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:longtime}), then $\chi$ small and $T$ large. The bootstrap assumptions (B1)--(B5), stated at the start of Appendix~\ref{app:patches}, hold at $t_0$ when $T$ is large: (B1) by \eqref{eq:slicebound}, since $|\bar x(t_0) - \sqrt{t_0}\,Y| \le \sqrt N\,r$, and (B2), (B3) and (B5) because the supports lie within $2r$ of the centres. Suppose that one of them fails at a first time $T_* < \infty$. The quantities involved are continuous in $t$, so the assumptions hold on $[t_0, T_*)$, and at $T_*$ with $\le$ in place of $<$.

\emph{Where $N = 3$ entered.} Zbarsky's proof uses three vortices in one place: to recover (B1), at the end of his Section~3. There he uses that the angular impulse and the energy of the centres are nearly conserved, and that for three vortices these two quantities, with the rotation and the dilation, are coordinates near the reference configuration \cite[Lemma~1(ii)]{zbarsky2021}; the dilation then follows from $d|\bar x_1|^2/dt$. For $N \ge 4$ the configurations modulo rotation and dilation have dimension $2N - 4 > 2$, and two nearly conserved quantities cannot pin them down. We use Proposition~\ref{prop:forced} instead. Two further facts enter, and both hold here: $\sum_{i<j}\Gamma_i\Gamma_j = 0$, exactly for our circulations (the program checks it in rational arithmetic; it is necessary for any self-similar expansion, since $H(\lambda\zeta) = H(\zeta) - \ln\lambda\sum_{i<j}\Gamma_i\Gamma_j$), which is used in Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration}; and $\sum_j\Gamma_j \ne 0$, which follows because $(\sum_j\Gamma_j)^2 = \sum_j\Gamma_j^2 + 2\sum_{i<j}\Gamma_i\Gamma_j > 0$ (the sums are $253/90$ and $93/40$); it is what lets a translation put the centre of vorticity at the origin, $\sum_j\int x\,\omega_j = 0$, which the appendix assumes.

\emph{(a) The centres move as point vortices up to a small error.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:centres}, with $V_i^0$ the velocity of the $i$th vortex in \eqref{eq:bs} for the circulations $-\Gamma$ at the positions $\bar x$,
\[
  \frac{d\bar x}{dt} = V_{-\Gamma}(\bar x) + e(t),\qquad |e(t)| \le C_et^{\varepsilon/2 - 3/2}\quad(t_0 \le t \le T_*),
\]
with $C_e$ depending on $Y$, $\varepsilon$, $M$ and $r$ but not on $T_*$.

\emph{(b) Recovering (B1).} Put $\zeta(s) = \rho^{-1}e^{ib^*s}\bar x(t)$ with $\rho = \sqrt{t/\pi}$ and $s = \ln\rho$. The computation after Proposition~\ref{prop:forced} uses only $d\rho/dt = 1/(2\pi\rho)$, so $\zeta' = -E(\zeta) + 2\pi\rho e^{ib^*s}e(t)$, and $2\pi\rho|e(t)| \le 2\pi C_e\rho(\pi\rho^2)^{\varepsilon/2 - 3/2} \le 2\pi C_ee^{-(2 - \varepsilon)s}$. In the shifted time $s - s_0$, $s_0 = \ln\rho(t_0)$, the hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:forced} holds with $\mu = 2 - \varepsilon$, forcing $\alpha_1 = 2\pi C_e\rho(t_0)^{\varepsilon - 2}$, and $\delta = \operatorname{dist}(\zeta(s_0), \mathcal O) \le |\bar x(t_0) - \sqrt{t_0}\,Y|/\rho(t_0) \le \sqrt{\pi N}\,r\,t_0^{-1/2}$. Both tend to $0$ as $t_0 \to \infty$, so for $T$ large the proposition, applied with $\nu = 1/2$ on $[s_0, \ln\rho(T_*))$ and extended to $T_*$ by continuity, gives $\operatorname{dist}(\zeta(s), \mathcal O) \le C_1(\delta + \alpha_1)$ on $[t_0, T_*]$. Since $\min_\phi|\bar x(t) - \sqrt t\,e^{i\phi}Y| = \sqrt{t/\pi}\,\operatorname{dist}(\zeta(s), \mathcal O)$, and $\bar x(t) \in \mathcal V$ because $\sum_j\Gamma_j\bar x_j = -\sum_j\int x\,\omega_j$ is conserved and vanishes at $t_0$, \eqref{eq:slicebound} with $\lambda = \sqrt t$ gives $|z - Y| \le 4C_1(\delta + \alpha_1)/\sqrt\pi$ and $|\gamma/\sqrt t - 1| \le C_1(\delta + \alpha_1)/(\sqrt\pi|Y|)$ on $[t_0, T_*]$. For $T$ large both bounds are less than $\varepsilon^2/2$, so (B1) holds on $[t_0, T_*]$ with strict inequalities, in particular at $T_*$.

\emph{(c) The other assumptions.} Propositions~\ref{prop:longtime} (if $T_* \ge t_0 + t_0^{9/10}$) and~\ref{prop:shorttime} (otherwise) give (B2), (B3) and (B5) at $T_*$ with strict inequalities, and (B4) holds because the vorticity is transported by a divergence-free field. This contradicts the choice of $T_*$, so every assumption holds for all $t \ge t_0$, which gives (i) and (ii).

\emph{(d) Part (iii).} Now the estimate of (a) holds for all $t \ge t_0$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:forced} applies on $[s_0, \infty)$ with $\mu = 2 - \varepsilon > 1$, and its last part gives $\sigma_\infty$ with $|\sigma_\infty| \le C_1(\delta + \alpha_1) < \varepsilon$ and $\phi_1$ with $|\zeta(s) - e^{i(\phi_1 - (b(\sigma_\infty) - b^*)(s - s_0))}\xi(\sigma_\infty)| \le C_1(\delta + \alpha_1)e^{-(s - s_0)}$. Multiplying by $\rho e^{-ib^*s}$, and using $\rho e^{-(s - s_0)} = \rho(t_0)$, gives $|\bar x(t) - Z(t)| \le C_1(\delta + \alpha_1)\rho(t_0) \le C_1(\sqrt N\,r + \alpha_1\rho(t_0))$, which is bounded, with $\phi_\infty = \phi_1 + (b(\sigma_\infty) - b^*)s_0$. $Z$ is a motion by the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:physical}.
\end{proof}

\section{Numerical illustration, controls and a sample}\label{sec:numerics}

\emph{Controls.} The same program certifies two controls, with the same side conditions. A four-vortex collapse with circulations $(1, -1003/1000, 13/10, \Gamma_4)$ has $c = -47.7346\ldots < 0$: its remaining pair is real, $k < 0$, and its reversal is unstable. A three-vortex collapse has $\tr((DE - I)^2) = 4 - 2b^2$ to within its enclosure, the contribution of the six forced eigenvalues alone, as Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum} requires for $N = 3$.

\emph{Direct integration} (binary64, DOP853, relative tolerance $10^{-12}$, and $10^{-13}$ for the longest run; not part of the proof). The four-vortex expansion of Corollary~\ref{cor:expansion}, started from the midpoint of the certified box, keeps its shape to $4\cdot10^{-15}$ while its size grows $178$-fold; a random perturbation of $10^{-5}$ in each coordinate (a shape deviation of $3.3\cdot10^{-5}$) stays below $2\cdot10^{-4}$, settling on a nearby member of the family, the neutral direction. Measured against the member with the same energy, found by least squares as Theorem~\ref{thm:nonlinear} prescribes, a perturbation of $10^{-4}$ in each coordinate has a shape deviation (modulo translation, rotation and scaling, so weaker than the quantity in the theorem) that decays like the inverse of the size. Followed from $5$-fold to $5\cdot10^8$-fold growth, deviation times size stays between $3.0\cdot10^{-4}$ and $6.3\cdot10^{-4}$ for four vortices, and between $3.1\cdot10^{-3}$ and $2.1\cdot10^{-2}$ for five, where two oscillating modes of nearly equal frequency beat with a period of about $24$ in $\ln\rho$. The deviation from $\zeta^*$ itself levels off, near $1.1\cdot10^{-3}$ and $5.8\cdot10^{-3}$, and $H$ is conserved to $4\cdot10^{-14}$. The reversal of the unstable control, perturbed by $10^{-8}$ in each coordinate, departs from its shape by $1.5\cdot10^{-3}$ when its size has grown eightfold and by $0.3$ at sixteenfold. A self-similar collapse, by contrast, departs from self-similarity in binary64 under round-off alone \cite{lk2015, cwr2026}.

\emph{How common.} A least-squares solver from random starts, in binary64 with fixed seeds, gave $342$ converged four-vortex collapses (not checked for duplicates), of which $52$ have $c > 0$, and $543$ five-vortex collapses, of which $32$ have every remaining exponent with positive real part (\texttt{survey\_\allowbreak expansions.py}). This is a sample of what a naive search finds, not a measure on the collapse set. Of the $52$ four-vortex cases, $37$ have the remaining pair on $\re k = 1$, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:four}, and $15$ a real pair $0 < k < 2$. Of the $32$ five-vortex cases, $10$ have both remaining pairs on $\re k = 1$, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:five}, $16$ a quadruple $1 \pm x \pm iy$ with $0 < x < 1$, and $6$ a real pair $0 < k < 2$.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The theorems give self-similarly expanding configurations of four and five vortices that are linearly asymptotically stable modulo the rotation and the family of expansions with the same circulations, with a spectral gap. Theorem~\ref{thm:nonlinear} makes this nonlinear: in similarity variables, nearby motions converge, up to a rotation drifting at the rate $b(\sigma_\infty) - b^*$ and at the rate the linearization predicts, to the member of the family with their own energy, and in the original variables they stay within a bounded distance of an exactly self-similar expansion. Zbarsky writes that orbital linear stability, ``which may not be hard to obtain for some systems, is not enough'' \cite{zbarsky2021}. What his argument needs is an estimate for approximate solutions whose error is small and decays, with constants that do not depend on the length of time, and Proposition~\ref{prop:forced} is such an estimate. Theorem~\ref{thm:patches} is then his theorem for these two configurations. Its proof follows his argument, and Appendix~\ref{app:patches} gives the estimates for $N$ patches in full: the other patches act on a patch only through the strain of their field, and a single renormalizing function built from that strain replaces his functions for the two other patches. Small slips of the arXiv version are corrected there; none affects his conclusions.

The examples are Euler vortices. The $\alpha$-models, where a vortex induces the velocity $\Gamma r^{-\alpha-1}/(2\pi)$, have the same structure after the obvious changes of homogeneity, and we have not treated them. Nor have we asked which fraction of the collapse set reverses into stable expansions, or whether stable expansions exist for every $N \ge 4$; the sample of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} suggests that they are common for $N = 4$ and $5$.

\appendix
\section{The estimates for the patches}\label{app:patches}

This appendix proves the estimates used in steps (a) and (c) of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:patches}, for any number $N \ge 3$ of patches (so many are needed for $\sum_{i<j}\Gamma_i\Gamma_j = 0$ with every $\Gamma_j \ne 0$) whose centres satisfy the bootstrap assumption (B1). The structure and the ideas are Zbarsky's \cite{zbarsky2021}: the moments $I_{n,j}$, their renormalization, the concentration of each patch by the conservation of energy, and the averaging of the distance of a point of a patch to its centre over one turn. The patches other than $\omega_i$ enter only through the field they induce near $\omega_i$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:field}), and a single renormalizing function, built from the strain matrix of that field, replaces the functions $f_{k,2}$, $f_{k,3}$ of \cite{zbarsky2021}, one for each other patch. We work in the units of \eqref{eq:bs}, $K(x) = x^\perp/(2\pi|x|^2)$, so that $u = K*\omega$.

\subsection{Notation and consequences of the bootstrap}

Put $\Omega_j = \int\omega_j = -\Gamma_j \ne 0$ and $m_j = \int|\omega_j| = |\Omega_j|$; each $\omega_j$ has the sign of $\Omega_j$, so $\omega_j = \operatorname{sgn}(\Omega_j)|\omega_j|$. Write $r_{\mathrm{in}}$ for the radius $r$ of the initial supports in Theorem~\ref{thm:patches}, since from now on $r$ denotes a distance: for a fixed $i$ write $\bar x = \bar x_i$, $r = r(x) = |x - \bar x_i|$, $I_n = I_{n,i} = \int r^n|\omega_i|$ and $m = m_i$. The bootstrap assumptions, with $\varepsilon \le 1/18$, an even integer $n \ge 4$ and $M$ as in the theorem, hold on $[t_0, T_*)$ as stated below and at $T_*$ with $\le$ in place of $<$:
(B1) $\bar x = e^{i\beta}\gamma z$ with $z \in S_Y$, $|z - Y| < \varepsilon^2$ and $|\gamma/\sqrt t - 1| < \varepsilon$;
(B2) $I_{2,j} < t^{\varepsilon/2}$;
(B3) $I_{n,j} < t^{n(1+\varepsilon)/4}$;
(B4) $\omega_j$ has the sign of $\Omega_j$ and $\|\omega_j\|_{L^{2+\varepsilon}} \le M$;
(B5) $\operatorname{supp}\omega_j \subseteq B(\bar x_j, \varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon})$.
Below, $C$ denotes constants that depend only on $Y$, $\Gamma$, $\varepsilon$, $M$, $r_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $n$, possibly different at each occurrence, and never on $\chi$, $R$, $t_0$ or $T_*$; $C_n$ records a dependence on $n$ where it matters. All statements hold for $t_0 \le t \le T_*$ and $t_0 \ge T$, with $T$ large enough, chosen last.

The solution is Yudovich's \cite{yudovich1963}: $u$ is bounded, log-Lipschitz in $x$ and continuous in $(t, x)$; its flow $X(t, a)$, the position at time $t$ of the particle that is at $a$ at time $t_0$, is for each $t$ a measure-preserving homeomorphism of $\R^2$, and it is $C^1$ in $t$ with $\partial_tX = u(t, X)$; and each $\omega_j(t)$ is $\omega_j(t_0)$ transported by $X$. For every $\phi \in C^1(\R\times\R^2)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
  \frac{d}{dt}\int\phi(t, x)\,|\omega_j(t, x)|\,dx = \int\bigl(\partial_t\phi + u\cdot\nabla\phi\bigr)|\omega_j|\,dx,
\end{equation}
and the same holds with $\omega_j$ in place of $|\omega_j|$, because the left side is the derivative of $\int\phi(t, X(t, a))|\omega_j(t_0, a)|\,da$ and $\operatorname{supp}\omega_j(t_0)$ is compact, so one may differentiate under the integral. With $\phi = x$, \eqref{eq:transport} gives $d\bar x_j/dt = \Omega_j^{-1}\int u\,\omega_j = \Omega_j^{-1}\int u(t, X(t, a))\,\omega_j(t_0, a)\,da$, which is continuous in $t$ because $u$ is continuous in $(t, x)$. So each centre $\bar x_j$ is $C^1$ in $t$, and the test functions below, which depend on $t$ through the centres, are admissible in \eqref{eq:transport}. $L^p$ norms and signs are preserved, so (B4) holds for all $t$. The other quantities in (B1)--(B5) are continuous in $t$: the moments by \eqref{eq:transport}, those in (B1) because $\bar x$ is continuous, and the radius $\max_{p \in \operatorname{supp}\omega_j(t)}|p - \bar x_j(t)|$ because $\operatorname{supp}\omega_j(t) = X(t, \operatorname{supp}\omega_j(t_0))$ and $X$ is uniformly continuous on compact subsets of $[t_0, \infty) \times \R^2$. As (B1)--(B3) and (B5) hold at $t_0$ with strict inequalities (proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:patches}), a first time $T_*$ at which one of them fails satisfies $T_* > t_0$, and they hold on $[t_0, T_*)$ and, with $\le$, at $T_*$.

Let $d_Y = \min_{i \ne j}|Y_i - Y_j|$, $D_Y = \max_j|Y_j|$ and $c_Y = d_Y/4$. By (B1), $|\bar x_i - \bar x_j| = \gamma|z_i - z_j|$ with $\gamma \in ((1-\varepsilon)\sqrt t, (1+\varepsilon)\sqrt t)$ and $|z_j - Y_j| < \varepsilon^2$, so for $\varepsilon$ small
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geom}
  2c_Y\sqrt t \le |\bar x_i - \bar x_j| \le 3D_Y\sqrt t,\qquad |\bar x_j| \le 2D_Y\sqrt t,
\end{equation}
and for $T$ large every $x \in B(\bar x_i, 2\varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon})$ and every $y \in B(\bar x_j, \varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon})$, $j \ne i$, satisfy $|x - y| \ge 2c_Y\sqrt t - 3\varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon} \ge c_Y\sqrt t$; by (B5) the second ball contains $\operatorname{supp}\omega_j$. We use $|K(z)| \le C|z|^{-1}$, $|\nabla K(z)| \le C|z|^{-2}$ and $|\nabla^2K(z)| \le C|z|^{-3}$.

\begin{lemma}[The field of the other patches]\label{lem:field}
Let $V_i = \sum_{j\ne i} K*\omega_j$. For $x \in B(\bar x_i, 2\varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon})$, $|V_i(x)| \le Ct^{-1/2}$, $|\nabla V_i(x)| \le Ct^{-1}$ and $|\nabla^2V_i(x)| \le Ct^{-3/2}$, and
\[
  V_i(x) = V_i^0 + \Lambda_i(x - \bar x_i) + \Theta_i(x),\qquad |\Theta_i(x)| \le Ct^{-3/2}\Bigl(|x - \bar x_i|^2 + \sum_jI_{2,j}\Bigr),
\]
with $V_i^0 = \sum_{j\ne i}\Omega_jK(\bar x_i - \bar x_j)$ and $\Lambda_i = \sum_{j\ne i}\Omega_j\nabla K(\bar x_i - \bar x_j)$. The matrix $\Lambda_i$ is symmetric with trace zero, and $|\Lambda_i| \le Ct^{-1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The bounds on $V_i$ and its derivatives follow from $|x - y| \ge c_Y\sqrt t$ on the supports. For $j \ne i$ and $y \in \operatorname{supp}\omega_j$, the segment from $x - \bar x_j$ to $x - y$ consists of the points $x - y'$ with $y' \in [\bar x_j, y] \subseteq B(\bar x_j, \varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon})$, so $|\nabla^2K| \le C(c_Y\sqrt t)^{-3}$ on it, and Taylor's formula gives $K(x - y) = K(x - \bar x_j) + \nabla K(x - \bar x_j)(\bar x_j - y) + O(t^{-3/2}|y - \bar x_j|^2)$; integrating against $\omega_j$, the linear term vanishes because $\bar x_j$ is the centre of vorticity, so $K*\omega_j(x) = \Omega_jK(x - \bar x_j) + O(t^{-3/2}I_{2,j})$. Expanding $K(x - \bar x_j)$ about $\bar x_i - \bar x_j$ in the same way, along the points $x' - \bar x_j$ with $x' \in [\bar x_i, x]$, gives the formula. With $G(z) = \log|z|/(2\pi)$ and $J$ the rotation by $\pi/2$, $K = J\nabla G$, so $\nabla K = J\nabla^2G$; $\nabla^2G$ is symmetric with trace $\Delta G = 0$, and for $S = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a & b\\ b & -a\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ one has $JS = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-b & a\\ a & b\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, again symmetric with trace zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The centres]\label{lem:centres}
$d\bar x_i/dt = V_i^0 + e_i(t)$ with $|e_i| \le Ct^{-3/2}\sum_jI_{2,j} \le Ct^{\varepsilon/2 - 3/2}$. Hence $|d\bar x_i/dt| \le Ct^{-1/2}$ and $|d\Lambda_i/dt| \le Ct^{-2}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:transport} with $\phi(x) = x$, $\Omega_i\,d\bar x_i/dt = \iint K(x - y)\omega_i(x)\omega_i(y)\,dx\,dy + \int V_i\omega_i$. The first integral converges absolutely, since $|K(z)| \le 1/(2\pi|z|)$ and $\omega_i \in L^1 \cap L^{2+\varepsilon}$, and vanishes because $K$ is odd. By Lemma~\ref{lem:field} the second is $\Omega_iV_i^0 + \Lambda_i\int(x - \bar x_i)\omega_i + \int \Theta_i\omega_i = \Omega_iV_i^0 + O(t^{-3/2}(I_{2,i} + m_i\sum_jI_{2,j}))$. Finally $|V_i^0| \le Ct^{-1/2}$, and $d\Lambda_i/dt = \sum_{j\ne i}\Omega_j\nabla^2K(\bar x_i - \bar x_j)[d\bar x_i/dt - d\bar x_j/dt]$ with $|\nabla^2K(\bar x_i - \bar x_j)| \le Ct^{-3/2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The moments}

Let $q(x) = (x - \bar x)\cdot \Lambda_i(x - \bar x)$, so that $|q| \le Ct^{-1}r^2$. For vectors $a$, $b$ write $[a, b] = a_1b_2 - a_2b_1$, so that $a\cdot b^\perp = -[a, b]$ and $[a, b] = [a, b - a]$.

\begin{lemma}[The moments]\label{lem:moments}
For every even $n \ge 2$,
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}I_n = A_n + n\int r^{n-2}q\,|\omega_i| + B_n,
\]
where $A_2 = 0$, $|A_n| \le C_nI_2I_n^{(n-4)/n}$ for $n \ge 4$, and $|B_n| \le Cnt^{-3/2}\bigl(\sup_{\operatorname{supp}\omega_i}r\cdot I_n + \sum_jI_{2,j}\cdot I_n^{(n-1)/n}\bigr)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:transport} to $\phi = |x - \bar x(t)|^n$, a polynomial in $x$: $dI_n/dt = n\int r^{n-2}(x - \bar x)\cdot(u(x) - d\bar x/dt)|\omega_i|\,dx$. Split $u = K*\omega_i + V_i$. The part of $K*\omega_i$ is $A_n = n\iint r_x^{n-2}(x - \bar x)\cdot K(x - y)|\omega_i(x)|\omega_i(y)\,dx\,dy$, with $r_x = r(x)$, $r_y = r(y)$. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:field} and~\ref{lem:centres}, $V_i(x) - d\bar x/dt = \Lambda_i(x - \bar x) + \Theta_i(x) - e_i$, which gives the middle term and $B_n = n\int r^{n-2}(x - \bar x)\cdot(\Theta_i - e_i)|\omega_i|$; the bound on $B_n$ follows with $I_{n-1} \le m^{1/n}I_n^{(n-1)/n}$ (H\"older's inequality).

For $A_n$, note $(x - \bar x)\cdot(x - y)^\perp = (x - \bar x)\cdot(\bar x - y)^\perp = [x - \bar x, y - \bar x]$. For $n = 2$ the integrand is $2\operatorname{sgn}(\Omega_i)[x - \bar x, y - \bar x]|x - y|^{-2}|\omega_i(x)||\omega_i(y)|/2\pi$, antisymmetric in $x$ and $y$ and absolutely integrable (it is at most $r_x|x - y|^{-1}$), so $A_2 = 0$. For $n \ge 4$, since $\int(\bar x - y)^\perp\omega_i(y)\,dy = 0$ we may subtract $r_x^{n-4}(x - \bar x)\cdot(\bar x - y)^\perp$ from the kernel, so
\[
  A_n = \frac{n}{2\pi}\iint\Phi(x, y)\,|\omega_i(x)|\,\omega_i(y)\,dx\,dy,\qquad \Phi = r_x^{n-2}(x - \bar x)\cdot(\bar x - y)^\perp\Bigl(\frac1{|x - y|^2} - \frac1{r_x^2}\Bigr).
\]
On $S_1 = \{r_x < r_y/2\}$, $|x - y| \ge r_y/2$ and $|\Phi| \le r_x^{n-1}r_y(4r_y^{-2} + r_x^{-2}) \le r_x^{n-4}r_y^2$. On $S_3 = \{r_x > 2r_y\}$, $|x - y| \ge r_x/2$ and $\bigl||x - y|^{-2} - r_x^{-2}\bigr| \le (2r_xr_y + r_y^2)\,4r_x^{-4} \le 10r_yr_x^{-3}$, so $|\Phi| \le 10r_x^{n-4}r_y^2$. On $S_2 = \{r_y/2 \le r_x \le 2r_y\}$ the subtracted term is at most $r_x^{n-3}r_y \le 2r_x^{n-4}r_y^2$; the other term, $r_x^{n-2}[x - \bar x, y - \bar x]|x - y|^{-2}$, has an antisymmetric numerator and $S_2$ is symmetric, so its integral equals half the integral of $(r_x^{n-2} - r_y^{n-2})[x - \bar x, y - \bar x]|x - y|^{-2}$. Here $r_x^{n-2} - r_y^{n-2} = (r_x^2 - r_y^2)P$ with $0 \le P \le \frac{n-2}{2}\max(r_x, r_y)^{n-4}$, $|r_x^2 - r_y^2| = |(x - y)\cdot(x + y - 2\bar x)| \le |x - y|(r_x + r_y)$ and $|[x - \bar x, y - \bar x]| = |[x - \bar x, y - x]| \le r_x|x - y|$, so this term is at most $C_nr_x^{n-4}r_y^2$ on $S_2$. Altogether $|A_n| \le C_n\iint r_x^{n-4}r_y^2|\omega_i(x)||\omega_i(y)| = C_nI_{n-4}I_2 \le C_nm^{4/n}I_n^{(n-4)/n}I_2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Concentration and renormalization}

\begin{lemma}[Concentration]\label{lem:concentration}
There is a constant $C_E$ such that $\iint|\omega_i(x)||\omega_i(y)|\log^+|x - y|\,dx\,dy \le C_E$ on $[t_0, T_*]$ for every $i$. Hence for every $\chi \in (0, 1)$ there is $R = R(\chi) \ge 1$, depending on $\chi$ and $C$, such that at every $t$ each patch has a point $\tilde x_i(t)$ with $\int_{|x - \tilde x_i| > R}|\omega_i|\,dx \le \chi^4m_i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The pseudo-energy $\mathcal E = \iint\log|x - y|\,\omega(x)\omega(y)\,dx\,dy$ is conserved for bounded compactly supported vorticity \cite{mp1994}. Write $\mathcal E = \sum_i\mathcal E_{ii} + \sum_{i\ne j}\mathcal E_{ij}$ with $\mathcal E_{ij} = \iint\log|x - y|\,\omega_i(x)\omega_j(y)$. For $i \ne j$, $x \in \operatorname{supp}\omega_i$ and $y \in \operatorname{supp}\omega_j$, by (B5) and \eqref{eq:geom} $\log|x - y| = \log|\bar x_i - \bar x_j| + O(t^{-1/4+\varepsilon})$, and by (B1) $\log|\bar x_i - \bar x_j| = \tfrac12\log t + \log|Y_i - Y_j| + O(\varepsilon)$. So $\sum_{i\ne j}\mathcal E_{ij} = \tfrac12\log t\sum_{i\ne j}\Omega_i\Omega_j + O(1) = O(1)$, because $\sum_{i\ne j}\Omega_i\Omega_j = 2\sum_{i<j}\Gamma_i\Gamma_j = 0$, uniformly in $t \in [t_0, T_*]$. At $t_0$ also $\mathcal E_{ii} = O(1)$, since the supports have radius at most $2r_{\mathrm{in}}$ and the negative part is bounded as below. By conservation, $\sum_i\mathcal E_{ii}(t) = O(1)$. Now $\mathcal E_{ii} = P_i - Q_i$ with $P_i = \iint|\omega_i||\omega_i|\log^+|x - y| \ge 0$ and, by H\"older's inequality in $y$ with exponents $2 + \varepsilon$ and $(2+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon)$,
\[
  0 \le Q_i = \iint|\omega_i(x)||\omega_i(y)|\log^-|x - y| \le m_i\|\omega_i\|_{L^{2+\varepsilon}}\bigl\|\log^-|\cdot|\bigr\|_{L^{(2+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon)}(B(0,1))} \le C.
\]
Hence $\sum_iP_i \le C$ and each $P_i \le C_E$. Take $R = \max(e, \exp(C_E/(\chi^4\min_jm_j^2)))$; then $\iint_{|x - y| > R}|\omega_i(x)||\omega_i(y)| \le C_E/\log R \le \chi^4m_i^2$, so the average over $y$, with weight $|\omega_i(y)|$, of $\int_{|x - y| > R}|\omega_i(x)|\,dx$ is at most $\chi^4m_i$, and some $y = \tilde x_i$ in the support does not exceed the average.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Renormalization]\label{lem:renormalization}
Let $F(t, x) = |x - \bar x|^n\,(x - \bar x)\cdot J\Lambda_i(x - \bar x)$, and let $\chi \in (0, 1)$ with $\chi^{3n-5} \le 16^{-n}$. For $t \ge t_\chi$,
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}\int F|\omega_i| = \frac{\Omega_i}{\pi}\int r^{n-2}q\,|\omega_i| + O\bigl(t^{-1}(\chi I_n + \chi^{-n}R(\chi)^n)\bigr),
\]
where the constant in $O$ does not depend on $\chi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$F$ is a polynomial in $x$ of degree $n + 2$ with $|F| \le Ct^{-1}r^{n+2}$, $|\nabla F| \le C_nt^{-1}r^{n+1}$ and $|\nabla^2F| \le C_nt^{-1}r^n$, since $|\Lambda_i| \le Ct^{-1}$. In polar coordinates about $\bar x$, with $\Lambda_i = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a & b\\ b & -a\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, one has $q = r^2(a\cos2\theta + b\sin2\theta)$ and $(x - \bar x)\cdot J\Lambda_i(x - \bar x) = r^2(a\sin2\theta - b\cos2\theta)$, so $\partial_\theta F = 2r^nq$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:principal}
  \nabla F(x)\cdot K(x - \bar x) = \frac{\partial_\theta F}{2\pi r^2} = \frac{r^{n-2}q}{\pi}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:transport}, $\frac{d}{dt}\int F|\omega_i| = \int\partial_tF|\omega_i| + \int\nabla F\cdot V_i|\omega_i| + \iint\nabla F(x)\cdot K(x - y)|\omega_i(x)|\omega_i(y)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:centres}, $|\partial_tF| \le C_nt^{-1}r^{n+1}|d\bar x/dt| + r^{n+2}|d\Lambda_i/dt| \le C_nt^{-1}(t^{-1/2}r^{n+1} + t^{-1}r^{n+2})$, and $|V_i| \le Ct^{-1/2}$; with $r \le \varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ on the support, the first two integrals are $O(t^{-1}\cdot t^{-1/4+\varepsilon}I_n)$, which is at most $t^{-1}\chi I_n$ for $t \ge t_\chi$. By \eqref{eq:principal} and $\int\omega_i = \Omega_i$, the double integral is
\[
  \frac{\Omega_i}{\pi}\int r^{n-2}q\,|\omega_i| + \iint\nabla F(x)\cdot\bigl(K(x - y) - K(x - \bar x)\bigr)|\omega_i(x)|\omega_i(y)\,dx\,dy.
\]
We bound the last integral on $S_4 = \{r_x \le \chi r_y\}$, $S_5 = \{\chi r_y < r_x < r_y/\chi\}$ and $S_6 = \{r_x \ge r_y/\chi\}$. On $S_6$, $|y - \bar x| \le \chi r_x$, so $|K(x - y) - K(x - \bar x)| \le Cr_y/r_x^2 \le C\chi/r_x$ and the integrand is at most $C_n\chi t^{-1}r_x^n$. On $S_4$, $|x - y| \ge r_y/2$, so $|\nabla F(x)\cdot K(x - y)| \le C_nt^{-1}r_x^{n+1}/r_y \le C_nt^{-1}r_x^n$, and $|\nabla F(x)\cdot K(x - \bar x)| \le C_nt^{-1}r_x^n$ on $S_4 \cup S_5$. On $S_5$, which is symmetric in $x$ and $y$, the integral of $\nabla F(x)\cdot K(x - y)$ against the symmetric weight $\operatorname{sgn}(\Omega_i)|\omega_i(x)||\omega_i(y)|$ equals half the integral of $(\nabla F(x) - \nabla F(y))\cdot K(x - y)$, and $|\nabla F(x) - \nabla F(y)| \le C_nt^{-1}|x - y|(r_x^n + r_y^n)$, because every point of the segment $[x, y]$ lies within $\max(r_x, r_y)$ of $\bar x$; by symmetry again the $r_y^n$ part has the same integral as the $r_x^n$ part. Altogether the last integral is at most $C_nt^{-1}\bigl(\chi mI_n + \mathcal S\bigr)$ with $\mathcal S = \iint_{S_4\cup S_5}r_x^n|\omega_i(x)||\omega_i(y)|$.

It remains to show $\mathcal S \le 2\chi mI_n + m^2(3R/\chi)^n$, with $\tilde x = \tilde x_i$ and $R$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration}. On $S_4\cup S_5$, $r_x < r_y/\chi$. If $a = |\tilde x - \bar x| < 2R$: where $r_y \ge 3R$, $|y - \tilde x| > R$, so the $y$-mass is at most $\chi^4m$ and this part is at most $\chi^4mI_n$; where $r_y < 3R$, $r_x < 3R/\chi$ and this part is at most $m^2(3R/\chi)^n$. If $a \ge 2R$: on $B(\tilde x, R)$, $(x - \bar x)\cdot(\tilde x - \bar x)/a \ge a/2$ and the mass is at least $(1 - \chi^4)m$, so $\int_{B(\tilde x, R)}(x - \bar x)|\omega_i| $ has length at least $ma/4$; as $\int(x - \bar x)|\omega_i| = 0$, $\int_{\R^2\setminus B(\tilde x, R)}r|\omega_i| \ge ma/4$, and the part of it where $r \le \varrho_0 = a/(8\chi^4)$ is at most $\varrho_0\chi^4m = ma/8$. By H\"older's inequality on $\{r > \varrho_0\}\setminus B(\tilde x, R)$, whose mass is at most $\chi^4m$, $ma/8 \le I_n^{1/n}(\chi^4m)^{(n-1)/n}$, that is $I_n \ge ma^n8^{-n}\chi^{-4(n-1)}$. Then the part of $\mathcal S$ where $r_x < 2a/\chi$ is at most $m^2(2a/\chi)^n \le m\,16^n\chi^{3n-4}I_n \le \chi mI_n$, and where $r_x \ge 2a/\chi$ we have $r_y > \chi r_x \ge 2a$, so $|y - \tilde x| > a \ge R$ and that part is at most $\chi^4mI_n$.
\end{proof}

Combining Lemmas~\ref{lem:moments} and~\ref{lem:renormalization}: for $t \ge t_\chi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renormalized}
  \frac{d}{dt}I_n = A_n + \frac{n\pi}{\Omega_i}\frac{d}{dt}\int F|\omega_i| + B_n + O\bigl(t^{-1}(\chi I_n + \chi^{-n}R(\chi)^n)\bigr),
  \qquad \Bigl|\int F|\omega_i|\Bigr| \le C\varepsilon^2t^{-1/2+2\varepsilon}I_n.
\end{equation}
Compared with \cite[(12)--(14)]{zbarsky2021}, the dependence of $F$ on $t$, through $\bar x$ and $\Lambda_i$, is accounted for by the term $\int\partial_tF|\omega_i|$ above, which the arXiv version omits from (34); the bound on $Q_i$ uses the exponents $2 + \varepsilon$ and $(2+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon)$ where the arXiv version writes an exponent below $1$. Neither changes the conclusions of \cite{zbarsky2021}.

\subsection{Long times}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:longtime}
Let $\chi$ be small enough, depending on $\varepsilon$, $n$ and $C$; in particular $\chi$ satisfies the hypothesis $\chi^{3n-5} \le 16^{-n}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:renormalization} and the same hypothesis with $2$ in place of $n$, which reads $\chi \le 1/256$, since the proof uses that lemma for both orders. Let $T$ be large enough, depending also on $\chi$. If $T_* \ge t_0 + t_0^{9/10}$ and (B1)--(B5) hold on $[t_0, T_*)$, and so with $\le$ at $T_*$, then at $T_*$, for every $i$, $I_{2,i} < T_*^{\varepsilon/2}$, $I_{n,i} < T_*^{n(1+\varepsilon)/4}$ and $\operatorname{supp}\omega_i \subseteq B(\bar x_i, \varepsilon T_*^{1/4+\varepsilon})$ with room to spare: every point of the support is at distance less than $\varepsilon T_*^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ from $\bar x_i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $t_1 = T_* - T_*^{2/3}$; then $t_1 \ge t_0$ and $T_*/2 \le t \le T_*$ on $[t_1, T_*]$. Integrate \eqref{eq:renormalized}, for the orders $2$ and $n$, over $[t_1, T_*]$ and use its second part at both ends. At $T_*$ the assumptions are used only with $\le$; the strict inequalities are used only at $t_1 < T_*$.

\emph{$n = 2$.} $A_2 = 0$ and, by (B2), (B5) and $I_1 \le m^{1/2}I_2^{1/2}$, $|B_2| \le Ct^{-5/4+2\varepsilon}$. Hence
\[
  I_2(T_*) \le I_2(t_1) + CT_*^{-1/2+5\varepsilon/2} + CT_*^{2/3}\bigl(T_*^{-5/4+2\varepsilon} + \chi T_*^{\varepsilon/2-1} + \chi^{-2}R^2T_*^{-1}\bigr).
\]
Since $(1 - x)^{\varepsilon/2} \le 1 - \varepsilon x/2$ for $0 \le x \le 1$, $I_2(t_1) < t_1^{\varepsilon/2} \le T_*^{\varepsilon/2} - \frac\varepsilon2T_*^{\varepsilon/2-1/3}$, and every other term is $o(T_*^{\varepsilon/2-1/3})$ except $C\chi T_*^{\varepsilon/2-1/3}$. So $I_2(T_*) < T_*^{\varepsilon/2}$ once $C\chi \le \varepsilon/4$ and $T$ is large.

\emph{$n \ge 4$.} Put $\ell = n(1+\varepsilon)/4 > 1$. By (B2), (B3) and (B5), $|A_n| \le C_nt^{\ell - 1 - \varepsilon/2}$ and $|B_n| \le Ct^{\ell - 5/4 + \varepsilon}$, so
\[
  I_n(T_*) \le I_n(t_1) + C\varepsilon^2T_*^{\ell - 1/2 + 2\varepsilon} + C_nT_*^{\ell - 1/3 - \varepsilon/2} + CT_*^{\ell - 7/12 + \varepsilon} + C\chi T_*^{\ell - 1/3} + C\chi^{-n}R^nT_*^{-1/3},
\]
and $I_n(t_1) < t_1^\ell \le T_*^\ell - T_*^{\ell - 1/3}$ because $(1 - x)^\ell \le 1 - x$. As $\varepsilon < 1/12$, $I_n(T_*) < T_*^\ell$ once $C\chi \le 1/2$ and $T$ is large.

\emph{The support.} Let $p_*$ be a point of $\operatorname{supp}\omega_i(T_*)$ and $p(t) = X(t; T_*, p_*)$, so that $p(t) \in \operatorname{supp}\omega_i(t)$; let $h = p - \bar x$ and $\varrho = |h|$. By (B5), $\varrho(t) \le \varepsilon t^{1/4+\varepsilon} \le 2\varrho_*$ on $[t_1, T_*]$, where $\varrho_* = \frac\varepsilon2T_*^{1/4+\varepsilon}$. Let $t_2 = \sup\{s \in [t_1, T_*] : s = t_1 \text{ or } \varrho(s) \le \varrho_*\}$. By continuity $\varrho(t_2) \le \varrho_*$ if $t_2 > t_1$, and $\varrho_* \le \varrho \le 2\varrho_*$ on $[t_2, T_*]$ if $t_2 < T_*$. If $t_2 = T_*$, then $\varrho(T_*) \le \varrho_*$ and there is nothing to prove, so let $t_2 < T_*$. On $[t_2, T_*]$, $\dot h = u(p) - d\bar x/dt = \Omega_iK(h) + \Lambda_ih + \eta$ with
\[
  \eta = w + \Theta_i(p) - e_i,\qquad w = \int\bigl(K(p - x) - K(h)\bigr)\omega_i(x)\,dx.
\]
We claim $|w| \le CT_*^{-3/4-5\varepsilon/2}$ once $n$ is large enough, depending on $\varepsilon$. Let $S_7 = \{r_x < \varrho/2\}$ and $S_8 = \{r_x \ge \varrho/2\}$, and $N_h = \nabla K(h)$. On $S_7$ the segment from $h$ to $p - x = h - (x - \bar x)$ stays at distance at least $\varrho/2$ from $0$, so $K(p - x) = K(h) - N_h(x - \bar x) + O(r_x^2\varrho^{-3})$, and $\int_{S_7}N_h(x - \bar x)\omega_i = -\int_{S_8}N_h(x - \bar x)\omega_i$ because $\bar x$ is the centre. So $|w| \le C\varrho^{-3}I_2 + \int_{S_8}(|K(p - x)| + |K(h)| + C\varrho^{-2}r_x)|\omega_i|$. By (B3), the mass of $S_8$ is at most $I_n(\varrho/2)^{-n} \le (4/\varepsilon)^nT_*^{-3n\varepsilon/4}$, and $\int_{S_8}r_x|\omega_i| \le I_n(\varrho/2)^{1-n}$. Fix $s = 2 + \varepsilon/2$ and $\varpi \in (0, 1)$ with $1/s = \varpi/(2+\varepsilon) + 1 - \varpi$; by H\"older's inequality and interpolation,
\[
  \int_{S_8}\frac{|\omega_i(x)|}{|p - x|}\,dx \le \bigl\||\cdot|^{-1}\bigr\|_{L^{s/(s-1)}(B(0,1))}M^\varpi\Bigl(\int_{S_8}|\omega_i|\Bigr)^{1-\varpi} + \int_{S_8}|\omega_i|.
\]
With $\varrho \ge \varrho_*$ and $I_2 \le T_*^{\varepsilon/2}$ the term $C\varrho^{-3}I_2$ in the bound on $|w|$ is at most $CT_*^{-3/4-5\varepsilon/2}$, and the integral over $S_8$ is at most $CT_*^{-3n\varepsilon(1-\varpi)/4}$, which is smaller once $3n\varepsilon(1-\varpi)/4 \ge 3/4 + 5\varepsilon/2$. Here $1 - \varpi = (\varepsilon/2)/((2 + \varepsilon/2)(1 + \varepsilon))$, which at $\varepsilon = 1/18$ is $18/1387$, so that the condition asks for $n \ge 1644$; this is harmless, since $n$ is fixed before $\chi$ and $T$ are chosen. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:field} and~\ref{lem:centres}, $|\Theta_i(p)| + |e_i| \le C\varepsilon^2T_*^{-1+2\varepsilon} \le CT_*^{-3/4-5\varepsilon/2}$ as $\varepsilon \le 1/18$. So $|\eta| \le CT_*^{-3/4-5\varepsilon/2}$.

Since $h\cdot K(h) = 0$ and $h^\perp\cdot K(h) = 1/2\pi$,
\[
  \frac{d\varrho^2}{dt} = 2q(p) + O(\varrho|\eta|),\qquad \dot\varphi = \frac{\Omega_i}{2\pi\varrho^2}\bigl(1 + \psi(t)\bigr),\quad |\psi| \le CT_*^{-1/2+2\varepsilon},
\]
where $\varphi$ is a continuous argument of $h$, because $|h^\perp\cdot(\Lambda_ih + \eta)|/\varrho^2 \le C(T_*^{-1} + |\eta|/\varrho)$. So $\varphi$ is strictly monotone, and it turns by $2\pi$ in time at most $\tau_1 = CT_*^{1/2+2\varepsilon}$; since $|\dot\varphi| \le |\Omega_i|(1 + |\psi|)/(2\pi\varrho_*^2)$ and $\varrho_*^2 = \frac{\varepsilon^2}4T_*^{1/2+2\varepsilon}$, each turn by $2\pi$ also takes time at least $T_*^{1/2+2\varepsilon}/C$. Divide $[t_2, T_*]$, of length at most $T_*^{2/3}$, into consecutive full turns, at most $CT_*^{2/3}/T_*^{1/2+2\varepsilon}$ of them, and a last interval shorter than $\tau_1$. On a full turn, change the variable to $\varphi$: with $q = \varrho^2(a\cos2\varphi + b\sin2\varphi)$,
\[
  \int q\,dt = \frac{2\pi}{\Omega_i}\int\varrho^4(a\cos2\varphi + b\sin2\varphi)(1 + \psi)^{-1}\,d\varphi.
\]
Over the turn, $\varrho^2$ changes by at most $\tau_1\cdot C(T_*^{-1}\varrho^2 + \varrho|\eta|) \le CT_*^{4\varepsilon}$ and $a$, $b$ by at most $\tau_1|d\Lambda_i/dt| \le CT_*^{-3/2+2\varepsilon}$, while $\varrho^4|\Lambda_i| \le CT_*^{4\varepsilon}$. Replacing $\varrho^4a$, $\varrho^4b$ and $\psi$ by their values at the start of the turn changes the integral by $O(T_*^{6\varepsilon-1/2})$, and with frozen coefficients it vanishes. So the full turns contribute at most $CT_*^{2/3-1/2-2\varepsilon}T_*^{6\varepsilon-1/2} = CT_*^{4\varepsilon-1/3}$ to $\varrho^2(T_*) - \varrho^2(t_2)$, the last interval at most $\tau_1\,Ct^{-1}(2\varrho_*)^2 \le CT_*^{4\varepsilon}$, and the term $O(\varrho|\eta|)$ at most $CT_*^{2/3}\varrho_* T_*^{-3/4-5\varepsilon/2} \le CT_*^{1/6-3\varepsilon/2}$. If $t_2 > t_1$, then $\varrho(t_2) \le \varrho_*$ and $\varrho(T_*)^2 \le \varrho_*^2 + o(T_*^{1/2+2\varepsilon}) < 4\varrho_*^2$. If $t_2 = t_1$, then $\varrho(t_1)^2 \le \varepsilon^2t_1^{1/2+2\varepsilon} \le \varepsilon^2T_*^{1/2+2\varepsilon} - \frac{\varepsilon^2}2T_*^{1/6+2\varepsilon}$, and the three contributions are $o(T_*^{1/6+2\varepsilon})$ because $\varepsilon < 1/12$; so again $\varrho(T_*) < \varepsilon T_*^{1/4+\varepsilon}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Short times}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:shorttime}
With the same choices, if $T_* < t_0 + t_0^{9/10}$ and (B1)--(B5) hold on $[t_0, T_*)$, and so with $\le$ at $T_*$, the conclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:longtime} holds at $T_*$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $t_0 \le t \le T_* \le 2t_0$. At $t_0$ the support of $\omega_i$ lies within $2r_{\mathrm{in}}$ of $\bar x_i(t_0)$, so $I_n(t_0) \le (2r_{\mathrm{in}})^nm$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} and $|q| \le Ct^{-1}r^2$, $dI_n/dt \le Cnt^{-1}I_n + |A_n| + |B_n|$. For $n = 2$ this gives $dI_2/dt \le Ct^{-1}I_2 + Ct^{-5/4+2\varepsilon}$, and since $\int_{t_0}^{T_*}Ct^{-1}\,dt \le Ct_0^{-1/10}$, Gronwall's inequality gives $I_2(T_*) \le 2(4r_{\mathrm{in}}^2m + 1) < T_*^{\varepsilon/2}$ for $T$ large. For $n \ge 4$, (B2), (B3) and (B5) give $dI_n/dt \le C_nt^{\ell-1}$, so $I_n(T_*) \le (2r_{\mathrm{in}})^nm + C_nt_0^{9/10}t_0^{\ell-1} < T_*^\ell$. For the support, with $p$, $h$, $\varrho$ and $\varrho_*$ as before and $\varrho(t_0) \le 2r_{\mathrm{in}} < \varrho_*$, let $t_2 = \sup\{s \in [t_0, T_*] : \varrho(s) \le \varrho_*\} > t_0$, so that $\varrho(t_2) \le \varrho_*$. If $t_2 = T_*$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise $\varrho \ge \varrho_*$ on $[t_2, T_*]$, the bound $|\eta| \le CT_*^{-3/4-5\varepsilon/2}$ holds there as before, and $|\Lambda_ih| \le CT_*^{-1}\varrho_*$, so $\varrho(T_*) \le \varrho_* + T_*^{9/10}C(T_*^{-3/4+\varepsilon}) < 2\varrho_* = \varepsilon T_*^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ for $T$ large.
\end{proof}
