Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Minimal Winding in the Self-Similar Collapse of Three Point Vortices and of Two Concentric Vortex Polygons", for publication in *Regular and Chaotic Dynamics*.

In a self-similar collapse, point vortices move on logarithmic spirals. The dimensionless product P = |ω₀|t_c measures how tightly they wind. For three vortices we determine the minimum of P for every ratio of circulations:

- Normalize the circulations to (1, μ, −μ/(1+μ)) with 0 < μ ≤ 1. Each orientation of the vortex triangle then gives one collapsing arc, and each arc has a unique minimum.
- The squared minima are roots of an explicit cubic in P².
- The smaller minimum increases strictly from √3/2 (as μ → 0) to √2 (at μ = 1).

Consequently every self-similar three-vortex collapse satisfies |ω₀|t_c > √3/2, and the bound is sharp; since every collapse of three point vortices in the plane is self-similar, the bound covers every such collapse. Equivalently, each vortex travels more than twice its initial distance from the collision point before the collapse. The bound also has a short direct proof from an elementary inequality. For two concentric regular n-gons we give P in closed form and its minimum over the relative rotation, and with a vortex of any circulation at their center the bound P > √3/2 persists for every n and is again sharp. A further theorem explains why the same constant appears in both families: a strong vortex with any number of weak, tight pairs of opposite sign has P ≥ √3/2 − o(1) as the pairs weaken, with near-equality only for one shape of the pairs.

The proofs are self-contained. The algebraic steps were verified in exact arithmetic, and the formulas for P, its minima and the vortex paths were checked against direct Biot–Savart computations in high-precision arithmetic.
