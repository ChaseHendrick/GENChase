Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Minimal Winding in the Self-Similar Collapse of Point Vortices", for publication in *Regular and Chaotic Dynamics*.

In a self-similar collapse, point vortices move on logarithmic spirals. The dimensionless number P, which is |ω₀|t_c for the Euler equation, measures how tightly they wind. For three vortices we determine the minimum of P for every ratio of circulations:

- Normalize the circulations to (1, μ, −μ/(1+μ)) with 0 < μ ≤ 1. Each orientation of the vortex triangle then gives one collapsing arc, and each arc has a unique minimum.
- The squared minima are roots of an explicit cubic in P².
- The smaller minimum increases strictly from √3/2 (as μ → 0) to √2 (at μ = 1).

Consequently every self-similar three-vortex collapse satisfies |ω₀|t_c > √3/2, and the bound is sharp; since every collapse of three point vortices in the plane is self-similar, the bound covers every such collapse. Equivalently, each vortex travels more than twice its initial distance from the collision point before the collapse. The bound also has a short direct proof from an elementary inequality. In the generalized Euler (α-)models, which include the surface quasi-geostrophic equation, every self-similar collapse of three vortices has P > √(3+α)/(2+α) for every α > −1, again with a sharp constant. For two concentric regular n-gons, with or without a vortex at their center, we give P in closed form and its minimum over the relative rotation, and the bound P > √3/2 persists for every n. A further theorem explains why the same constant appears in these families: a strong vortex with any number of weak, tight pairs of opposite sign has P ≥ √3/2 − o(1) as the pairs weaken.

For more vortices the bound fails, and computer-assisted proofs in interval arithmetic show how: four, five and six Euler vortices can collapse self-similarly with P < √3/2, P has strict local minima on these collapses, and in the α = 2 model eleven vortices can collapse without rotating at all. Numerical results for larger numbers of vortices are reported as such.

The proofs are self-contained. The algebraic steps were verified in exact arithmetic, the formulas for P, its minima and the vortex paths were checked against direct Biot–Savart computations in high-precision arithmetic, and the programs of the computer-assisted proofs are published with the paper.
