Rotation, collapse, and scattering of point vortices

Abstract Relative equilibria, collapse, and scattering of point vortices in the plane are studied. Vortex systems
with arbitrary choice of circulations are considered. An ordinary differential equation satisﬁed by polynomials
with roots at the vortex positions is found. Explicit expressions for vortex double-ring conﬁgurations in the
cases of two regular polygons, three regular polygons, and a quadrangle with a digon are obtained.
Keywords Point vortices · Relative equilibrium · Collapse · Scattering
1 Introduction
The Helmholtz’s system describing the motion of point vortices in the plane has been intensively studied in
recent years [1–14]. This system induces complicated dynamics and is not integrable in the case of four or more
vortices with generic choice of circulations [15,16]. That is why, some particular motions such as rotation,
collapse, and scattering are of great interest. Relative equilibrium conﬁgurations were observed in superﬂuid
Helium, plasmas, and hurricanes (see [1,10] and references therein). The collapse phenomenon may turn to
be a mechanism contributing to the emergence of large atmospheric whirlwinds [17]. Analytic description of
relative equilibrium, collapse, and scattering conﬁgurations is a difﬁcult problem.
It is known since Thomson that M identical point vortices placed at the vertices of a regular M–gon are in
relative equilibrium [18]. Relative equilibria of identical point vortices located at the vertices of two and three
regular polygons with or without a vortex at the origin were constructed explicitly [2]. Less is known about
such conﬁgurations if the circulations of the vortices differ from polygon to polygon. Aref studied the case of
two polygons without a vortex at the origin [1]. O’Neil [5] established ﬁniteness of conﬁgurations with three
regular polygons whenever certain restrictions on circulations hold. In recent work, Celli et al. [10] showed
that point vortices with different circulations located at the vertices of a regular polygon cannot be in relative
equilibrium, when the number of vortices is more than three (two if there is an addition vortex at the origin).
Relative equilibria of identical point vortices placed on two perpendicular lines (the x and y axes) were
investigated by Aref et al. [12]. The general motion of point vortices located at the vertices of two concentric
regular M–gons was studied by Aref [19]. Further, the Aref’s results were developed by Koiller et al. [20].
Only few collapse conﬁgurations are known explicitly. Novikov and Sedov constructed collapse conﬁgu-
rations containing three point vortices or four point vortices with certain choices of circulations and derived
necessary conditions for the existence of homogeneous collapse conﬁgurations in the general case [17]. O’Neil

In this article, we study the problem of ﬁnding relative equilibrium, collapse, and scattering conﬁgurations
of point vortices with arbitrary choice of circulations. We derive an ordinary differential equation satisﬁed
by polynomials with roots at the vortex positions. This equation enables one to calculate the conﬁgurations
explicitly what is much simpler than direct solution of the Helmholtz’s system.
This article is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, we consider multivortex systems in the plane and obtain a
differential equation satisﬁed by generating polynomials describing relative equilibria, collapse, and scattering.
In Sects. 3–5, we present several explicit examples.

6 Conclusion
In this article, we have studied the problem of ﬁnding relative equilibrium, collapse, and scattering conﬁgura-
tions of point vortices with generic choice of circulations. We have found a differential equation satisﬁed by
generating polynomials of vortex arrangements. This equation enables one to calculate any such conﬁguration
explicitly. In detail, we have considered vortex double-ring conﬁgurations in the cases of two regular polygons,
three regular polygons, and a quadrangle with a digon. We have found a number of new conﬁgurations.
