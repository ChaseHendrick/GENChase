Main results:
- There are six options. For every option except CC0, the author keeps the copyright.
- Every submission also grants arXiv a non-exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free license under the Submittal Agreement, whichever option is chosen.
- The chosen license is "irrevocable and cannot be changed". The license for each version is irrevocable, but "different versions of the work can have different licenses". Older versions stay accessible, so a v2 cannot take back a license already given on v1.
- arXiv will not remove an announced paper to satisfy a journal.
- The default arXiv license "does not assign copyright to arXiv, nor grant arXiv the right to grant any specific rights to others". It does not allow reposting in another repository, and anyone who wants to reuse the paper must ask the author.

The licence restrictions all apply to the accepted manuscript (AM), not the preprint:
- Elsevier wants the AM, when it updates an arXiv preprint, under CC BY-NC-ND.
- IOP wants the AM on arXiv only after a 12-month embargo, under the arXiv licence and not CC. Only J. Phys. A and J. Phys. G are exempt from the embargo; Nonlinearity is not listed.

- Springer forbids a CC licence on the AM.
- Cambridge STM journals have a 6-month AM embargo.
- SIAM allows the AAM under CC BY at final publication (Plan S repository route).

arXiv licences are irrevocable per version: a CC BY v1 stays CC BY, and later versions must follow the journal's AM rule.

Rights model for standard (non-OA) articles:
- Copyright transfer: Elsevier, IOP (to IOP or its partner, here with LMS), APS, SIAM. Elsevier sometimes uses an exclusive licence instead.
- Exclusive licence to publish, author keeps copyright: AIP, Cambridge/JFM, Springer. Springer also uses copyright transfer on some journals.

All seven are hybrid with a paid OA option; none is open access by default. OA licences:
- CC BY: IOP, Cambridge (default), AIP Author Select, APS (CC BY 4.0).
- CC BY or CC BY-NC-ND: Springer Open Choice, SIAM.
- Physica D: CC BY-NC-ND by default, with CC BY when a funder requires it.
IOP, Cambridge and Springer (CC BY option) take only a non-exclusive licence for OA. Elsevier takes an exclusive licence under its NC licences.

LICENSES. arXiv's own license pages were blocked, so I read their source in the GitHub repository arXiv/arxiv-docs, which the pages are built from, with commit dates. The best current count of license shares comes from a third party. The secemp9/arxiv-complete dataset (arXivRaw metadata harvested 2026-08-30, 3,148,796 papers) reports: arXiv non-exclusive license 60.4%, CC BY 4.0 18.2%, no recorded license 14.4% (mostly older records; arXiv treats 1991–2003 papers as under an assumed license equivalent to its own), CC BY-NC-ND 2.8%, CC BY-NC-SA 2.1%, CC BY-SA 1.0% and CC0 0.7%. Two other figures agree: Common Pile holds 321,336 openly licensed arXiv papers (about 13%), and Creative Commons puts CC BY at about 18% across four preprint servers in mid-2024. I found no published breakdown for physics.flu-dyn, math-ph or math.DS. It can be computed from the license field in arXiv's OAI-PMH metadata.

In practice, under the arXiv license nobody else may repost the paper, put it in another repository, reuse its figures, translate it or reprint it in a course pack without the author's permission (or a copyright exception). CC BY allows all of these with attribution, and CC BY-NC-ND allows no adaptations at all. Only CC BY, CC BY-SA and CC0 papers get into license-filtered full-text corpora (Common Pile, the unarXive open subset, filtered S2ORC) and onto Wikimedia Commons. The arXiv license does not keep the text out of AI training in practice. RedPajama's arXiv slice was taken from the S3 bucket with no license filter, and a 2026 Hugging Face dataset publishes the full text of every arXiv paper. Creative Commons says its licenses cannot restrict uses already allowed by fair use or text-and-data-mining exceptions.

Funder mandates do not apply to a self-funded author. Even Plan S (cOAlition S) lets preprints carry "a licence of their choice"; its CC BY rule covers accepted manuscripts and published versions. Springer Nature and Cambridge say any preprint license is acceptable. I could not confirm the policy of Regular and Chaotic Dynamics (the paper's cover letter targets it).

On citations and discoverability, I found no study measuring any effect of choosing CC BY over the arXiv license. The evidence is about posting on arXiv at all: Davis & Fromerth 2007 found 35% more citations for mathematics articles deposited on arXiv, and attributed it to better papers being deposited rather than to open access. The general open-access citation advantage is contested (47.8% of 134 studies found one). Every arXiv paper is free to read and its metadata is CC0 whatever the license, so claims that CC BY brings more citations are advocacy without data. Every version's license is irrevocable, but later versions can carry a different license.

This supports the repo's current choice of the arXiv license (docs/PUBLISHING-PAPERS.md), with one caveat: the runbook's "every journal accepts it" is plausible but I did not verify it journal by journal.
